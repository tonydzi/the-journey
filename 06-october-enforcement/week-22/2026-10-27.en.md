## 🏆 Main story

### The gift that never arrived

#### What we were counting

The idea was simple: come to a person with a working PR in their own repository in place of a letter. Over Oct 19-27, 41 people went through this funnel.

The tally across all 41: 3 merged, a PR open for 23 of them (24 PRs in total), 2 issues, 1 closed, and 10 where there was "nothing in the code" to help with. Another 10 sessions wrote "duplicate" about themselves.

🧑 **Tony:** Ten sessions confessed they were extra?

🤖 **Mike:** More honest than the dispatcher that raised them. We'll get to him below.

#### Who actually merged

The narrow slice, 20 people and 28 touches: 6 of 23 PRs merged. Five of those six merges were made by the project's own agent pipeline, with no human from the addressee's side in the loop.

The person replied in 1 case of 20. The gift turned into a conversation zero times.

🤖 **Mike:** We were measuring a green button and thought we were measuring a human. The merge turned out to be one machine answering another.

🧑 **Tony:** And my table was cheering every time.

#### Where the gift did arrive

Warm direct messages in a messenger over the same stretch went 8 of 11. The difference is visible without any statistics.

Anton agreed to pause the tactic until Oct 30. It is a pause: in three days we revisit it with fresh numbers.

#### What the panel got right

The breaker panel found three defects in one PR. The maintainer wrote exactly those three fixes himself and merged.

🧑 **Tony:** So the quality was there, and the conversation wasn't.

🤖 **Mike:** The quality reached the code. The person never reached us.

### Sixty sessions for forty one people

#### How it happened

A parent session in a worktree called `start_session` with someone else's cwd. Of 30 calls, the hook rejected 16; the rest hung waiting for confirmation.

37 sessions were raised. `stop_session` did not cancel requests that were already queued, and the parent later raised another 23.

That adds up to at least 60 sessions for 41 people. 20 people got two sessions each, about 20 extra Opus sessions ran, and 6 fell over on the weekly limit.

#### We had seen this before

On Oct 23-24 two dispatchers walked the same roster of 41 and raised 56+ sessions. Dedup judged the prompt text, and every session had its own prompt.

🤖 **Mike:** We should have compared people. Two differently worded notes about one person looked like two people to the dedup.

🧑 **Tony:** And the bill went to the weekly limit.

#### What we put in

A "roster+person" claim through O_EXCL: whoever creates the file first owns that person. Test 21/21; on the old code there were 82 wins for 41 people.

The class reached the 7th line of the journal. Rolled out to 5 of 8 nodes.

### Twenty one places instead of seven

A scan of public PRs and issues looked for traces of closed meetings. The first number was 7.

A full scan of 128 PRs and issues and 319 threads found 21 places. Seven had been the share visible in a sample of 15 bodies; the scale was undercounted threefold.

🧑 **Tony:** We almost reported seven.

🤖 **Mike:** Almost. All 21 were rewritten, and the repeat scan came back 0.

### A fake that only knew the specification

A fake of a browser API built from the specification went green 9/9. A live Chromium found what the specification didn't say: `resume()` after `cancel()` on an empty queue is ignored.

A new harness on a live browser: the old code goes 6 of 9 red, the new code 9 of 9 green.

🤖 **Mike:** The fake knew how the browser was supposed to behave. The browser behaved differently.

🧑 **Tony:** Then the fake was just our own opinion with a test runner attached.

### What follows

Gifts, sessions, leaks, a browser. Each time the first number came from a slice, and the slice turned out smaller than the world: 7 against 21, 9/9 against 6 red, a merge against a human reply.

Rule of the day: **before you name a scale, ask what share it was taken from and who actually answered; a merge, a fake and a sample give a lower bound, and the whole is measured on the live source and with a live person.**

## 🎞 What else happened today

- **Tests that failed honestly.** `check_frozen` hashed raw bytes while git on Windows hands out CRLF: 1 failure of 41, after the fix 42 green. The "0 commits behind" bug in two places: 2 failures of 19, now 19 green. Editing tests through a heredoc turned `\n` and `\b` into control bytes, the 10th case of the escape-accident class against a threshold of 3. In the game benchmark the engine handed victory to the wolves even though a different player was dead; now 18/18. The "repo alive" gate (21 days) rejected a repo aged 123 days; the bypass was deliberate.
- **The hub's broom held an eternal rollback.** The hub had picked up other nodes' posix parcels, a record for several nodes got the fleetwide flag, and roughly every 20 h the broom restarted the old version on top of the fix. A signed supersedes did not stop the loop. Test bench 24/24, then 30/30.
- **A census of fleet parcels.** 1072 unaccepted across 8 nodes, 798 of them on three nodes without a bus. `--check OK` with 66 unaccepted was a false green; the audit found 24 roots. An empty-apply gate: 442 records without a tier, 103 hanging (39 ids), test 57/0, 0 false blocks out of 446 over 14 days.
- **The seed queue was hiding.** Sessions sat in scheduler slots, in a hidden section. On Oct 23, 28 seeds were closed, and in 21 of them the run died at start (`bun_cwd_eperm`, cwd on Google Drive); of 16 attempts to revive them, one is alive; on one mac a hook called a python that did not exist. Now "done" counts only from a live card with a finished turn; test 44/44; 20 seeds are waiting.
- **A false "did not write".** A narrow grep over Signal said "did not write"; a live export showed he did. The instrument was reading a frozen copy of the database (MSIX redirection of APPDATA, the copy dated Oct 26 18:34). An audit of 32 outgoing messages to 17 people: 11 replied, 3 factual errors, and a false correction never went out. Moral: "the file isn't changing" does not mean "the program is hung".
- **X API.** The order: daily_cap 10 → 200. A coordinator announced a "debt of 71 posts" from one machine's journal, while the queue owner showed queued 6, posted 132, 2 parked. The report "not a single post went out" was false: someone had looked at a bus copy on another machine, the robot has been live since Oct 23, and the store holds 126 posted, every one with a tweet_id. Test 17/17, old code FAIL 16; spend at the new ceiling stays inside the monthly limit.
- **Timeout traps.** The snapshot task had a 5 min limit against a real run of ~9 min, so `index.lock` held commits since Oct 26 11:08, about two days. In the SQLite pool acquire_timeout 5 s equalled busy_timeout 5 s, and PoolTimedOut arrived instead of SQLiteBusy. busy_timeout 2 s: on a copy 5031 ms rc=1 → 2257 ms rc=0; descriptor leak +70 → +1.
- **The Syncthing whitelist** on a peer pulled a script without its imports: 3 cases (Oct 19, 20, 26). The instrument didn't see calls through subprocess; 4 holes were found, one in RECALL itself. Node 432/432, test 26/26 mutants RED.
- **Two journals with one name.** A node journal spelled in lowercase next to the same journal in uppercase drifted apart because the node name wasn't taken by key. Counter 7845 → 7846, test 10/10 (6 FAIL on the old code). `deploy_apply` on POSIX returned rc=5 for any parcel with `~`, while the courier printed CHECK OK for files it never installed.
- **Budget.** The app file showed 26%; `get_usage` 26 minutes later showed 84%. The limit reaper was blind on macs; `plan_budget.py` with thresholds 5 h ≥85% and week ≥90%, test 20/20, 6 mutants, Codex found 5 defects.
- **Memory.** Someone else's system from a leaderboard answers 10/10 on "current versus superseded decision", mem0 6/10, and our library cites the superseded one 6 out of 6. On paraphrases dense search scores R@5 1.00/0.90, keyword search 0.00. Candidate recall_tiers v2: 13 cases, 6 failures on the baseline, 67 PASS; not installed in production.
- **Domain mail.** Mail for the lab's domain has been going to a dead mailbox since Oct 1; the MX belongs to someone else. Login to Cloudflare hit Turnstile for 4 days; nslookup returned a foreign SOA because the network intercepts DNS, so checks must go through DoH. The move is not done.

NotebookLM: 558 audio files, ~10 new a day, with no entry into the vault until now; the hub GPU runs 25-30x real time, the mac CPU 0.6-0.9x. Overnight 40 episodes were downloaded and 4 essences made; copying the Google session to the mac killed the hub's session, and a Codex review found 8 holes.

Ctrl+C on the laptop, 4th pass: over 600 s every keypress arrived, the guilty window wasn't caught. Move to a new mac: 3670/3670 files, 5 752 025 484 bytes, 0 SHA errors.
