## 🎬 Cold-open

Five robot Firefox profiles hold the same human Google SID.

The robots go to NotebookLM on their own business. The human in the next window could be thrown out of their mail at any moment.

There is a protection: the old flag `NOTEBOOKLM_DISABLE_KEEPALIVE_POKE`. It turns off the session keepalive poke, and that is true.

The agent traces where else the package goes. Session recovery calls take their own road, past the flag, and reach `RotateCookies`.

🧑 **Tony:** So the flag was set?

🤖 **Mike:** Set and working. It closed one door out of several, and the cookie does not rotate in the package. It rotates on the way out to the network.

## 🏆 The main story

### A flag in the package, a cookie in the network

#### What was at risk

Robot profiles took the human Firefox SID, five profiles. If a robot rotates the cookie, the human session can become invalid. To the human it looks like a sudden logout from Gmail.

The wide task about Gmail logouts is open. Today one specific path in it was closed off.

#### Why the old lock was green

`NOTEBOOKLM_DISABLE_KEEPALIVE_POKE` honestly disabled keepalive. The check "flag is set" passed, the keepalive test passed.

But the harm did not live in keepalive. Recovery calls inside the same package reached `RotateCookies` by their own road.

🧑 **Tony:** We checked that the flag was set, not that the cookie did not rotate?

🤖 **Mike:** Yes. The green was about the flag, and we read it as green about the cookie.

#### Where the lock went

Not inside the package, but at the process's network egress. `sitecustomize.py` loads with the interpreter before any other code and closes requests to `accounts.google.com/RotateCookies` fail-closed. Whatever path inside the package wants to rotate the cookie, it will not reach the network.

A second lock next to it: importing human cookies into robot profiles is blocked. The skill moved to version 1.2.1.

#### How it was proven

Tests 14 of 14 in the venv and 14 of 14 on the system Python. Two interpreters, because `sitecustomize.py` lives at the interpreter level, and a lock in one does not mean a lock in the other.

The main evidence is on the real wheel 0.8.5. The guard let 0 transport calls through. The control bypass, with the guard deliberately removed, let 2 through.

🤖 **Mike:** Without the control, "0 let through" could mean "there were no calls at all". A control with two let through proves the calls exist and the guard catches them.

The surrounding setup is alive: 240 notebooks, queue 177 of 177.

#### What is missing

Selfheal 2 of 3, the outside panel 0 of 6 answers. The wide Gmail-logout task is not closed: this path was one possible cause, not a proven only cause.

🧑 **Tony:** So I can still get logged out?

🤖 **Mike:** You can. One known road is now blocked on the lower floor. Nobody has walked the other floors.

### A mirror that was green on the old text

#### Numbers

Codex reads the canon through a mirror, not directly. Source v6.8.3, 90,380 bytes. The live global mirror v6.8.1, 98,280 bytes, with 24 bytes left before the ceiling.

The mirror's readiness check said "green".

🧑 **Tony:** Twenty four bytes from the wall, two versions behind, and all green?

🤖 **Mike:** The check looked at the stamp. The stamp was intact. Whether old text lay behind it, the check never asked.

#### Root

Each mirror consumer checked an incomplete stamp, and they had no shared contract. Each was right on its own layer. None compared the mirror against the hash of the current source.

Two more roots nearby. Parallel runs shared one staging area. And a secret was baked straight into the guard.

#### The secret in history

The secret literal sits in 13 of 14 old Git revisions. After a disk threshold error, Syncthing indexed the old versions.

Hence a targeted Syncthing scan in the list of fixes: you have to search not only current files but what the sync has remembered.

#### What was built

One readiness contract: source hash plus the C1-C10 adapter. An OS lock instead of shared staging, sealed bundles, a key loader instead of a literal.

Mirror candidate 97,217 bytes. Coverage map: 253 spans, of which 212 MERGE, 21 DROP, 20 KEEP, plus 7 negative controls. Behavioural scenarios: 0.

🤖 **Mike:** The map says which paragraph moved where. Whether Codex behaves on the new text the way it did on the old one, the map does not say. Zero scenarios, and that is an honest number.

#### What is missing

Five nodes read, three of them gave no bytes. Panel 0 of 6, the global mirror not published, Cursor has not confirmed acceptance.

Today a contract was made, not a mirror. The old mirror is still the live one.

### The package delivered, the model wrong

#### The order of the twelfth

On October 12 Anton gave an order about the default Codex model. It was counted as rolled out.

A per-node check showed otherwise: one node on ultra, one on high, one with no pin at all.

#### Where the green was

The package with the setting was delivered, and there were delivery receipts. Delivery is the upper layer.

The lower layer is the argv a launcher actually starts Codex with. Nobody looked there.

🧑 **Tony:** Why is this not day 133, where the fix never arrived?

🤖 **Mike:** There the parcel did not arrive, and that was visible. Here the parcel arrived, and that was visible too. The green delivery receipt was read as a green installation.

#### After

A narrow installer with a lease, a backup and a read-back of the written file's hash. One resolver, `codex_defaults`, so launchers stop keeping their own copies.

Result by layer: configs 5 of 5, CLI canaries 5 of 5, launcher argv 15 of 15, remote applications 3 of 3. The canaries consumed 55,144 + 26,019 + 57,756 + 42,687 + 32,091 input tokens, 213,697 in total.

#### What is missing

The canon text still says strongest/max. Robot launchers are unchecked.

Anton's current order: Frontier-minus-one Sol, medium, not Astra and not max. The canon lags behind the order.

🤖 **Mike:** Five of five configs and a law text that says the opposite. The next installer will read the law and rewrite all five.

### A distiller that measures shape

Session distillation was checked on three "source - summary" pairs. Errors were found in all three.

Source bodies of 4,451, 9,049 and 31,492 characters against a 30,000 cap. The third lost its last 1,492 characters, and the summary does not know it.

The worker checks the exit code, the length and the presence of TITLE and BODY fields. It does not check meaning. The router runs six rails and does not record which one won.

🧑 **Tony:** So "the summary is fine" means "the summary has a title"?

🤖 **Mike:** Exactly. Shape is green, nobody measured meaning.

In a window of 60 winners: the lighthouse 26, Gemini 17, Mistral 17, Grok 0. The reporter covers 156 of 243 human sessions, 64.2%.

One more layer down: the scheduler showed result 0 while the worker exited with 1. The scheduler's green describes the wrapper's launch, not the work.

No fix, only proposals. The real error rate is not measured: three pairs out of three is a sample, not a percentage.

### Harm inside a system process

#### The agent doorbell

The agent doorbell database was 0 bytes, the scheduler returned rc3, all 4 agents were UNKNOWN. An unknown author zeroed it on October 2.

After the fix 1 agent of 4 confirmed, `agent_acked` at 19:29:54Z. The other three are still silent.

#### audiodg handles

The recorder leaks handles, but they pile up not in the recorder, in the system audiodg. About +464 handles per 30 seconds, starting near 304, peaking at 139,426.

Root: the device format query (`IsFormatSupported`) in screenpipe leaks handles, and the app periodically re-enumerates devices. Each enumeration leaves a trace in someone else's process.

🤖 **Mike:** Look at the recorder and it is fine. The process swelling up is one the recorder does not own. The harm is a floor below where we would have searched.

The new path gives +0 against +500 for the old one on 13 of 13 devices. With the app paused, +2 in 33 seconds.

#### A build that finished

The CPU Whisper build ran 215.04 seconds, 428.3 CPU seconds, 2.315 GB RSS. Zero transcripts.

The Vulkan build: rc0 in 304.391 seconds, 25 audio_chunks and 8 transcripts. Later the Vulkan CLI build crashed in 18.75 seconds with rc101.

The installed screenpipe is still not fixed, after about five builds. A licence for running our own build permanently is not confirmed.

🧑 **Tony:** The build "passed" and not one word?

🤖 **Mike:** It passed as a process. It did not pass as a transcriber. Those are two different floors.
