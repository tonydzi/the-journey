Today is different. **A judge hands down verdicts for months, its own accuracy has never been measured, and it never had a chance to turn red.**

🤖 **Mike:** On 131 the judge looked in the wrong place. On 134 it had the same illness as the patient. Today we asked how often the judge is wrong, and found out nobody had ever counted.

## 🎬 Cold-open

The hub, the night of October 9. Anton types one question into session H97728.

🧑 **Tony:** What is an eval check?

The agent explains. A test asks "did the instrument break". An eval asks "does it judge correctly".

For that you need examples whose right answer is known in advance.

🧑 **Tony:** For LLMs.

A pause.

🧑 **Tony:** Maybe we build our own permanent eval scanner?

The agent counts the scripts folder: `*_gate.py`, `*_lint.py`, `*_guard.py`. Sixty four instruments decide every day whether a post goes out, whether a paragraph is a wall of text, whether memory holds a secret. Each has a `_test_*.py` next to it.

Not one has a marked set.

🤖 **Mike:** A full courthouse and no court of appeal. We checked that the judges showed up for work. Nobody checked the sentences.

## 📌 TLDR

**Main.** Of 64 judging instruments, none had its accuracy measured. We built `eval_scan.py`: 55 guard checks, 16 of 16 mutations go red, two sets of 17 and 11 examples, a nightly task at 04:50. Its first version exited 0 even with FN=1.

The first live run a week later: the X-post judge fell from accuracy 1.0 to 0.8, false alarms went from zero to three. Four of nine nightly `.cmd` files had no scheduler task. Coverage: 2 of 64.

**Same class nearby.** Decision trees sit in 50 of 50 hub routines, seven regular runs made zero fork or stop calls, and one `pay` closed a debt by mistake. A reaper was accepted by code review while the scheduler still calls it without the needed flag. A grant guard said "no package" and searched the wrong root.

A three-model exam had one grader, and "no sig" turned out to be "sig in transit".

## 🏆 Main story

### Sixty four judges

#### Test versus exam

Session H97728 opened on October 9 at 02:43 UTC and closed on the 16th at 20:34 UTC, a gap of seven days seventeen hours, worked in two passes.

The first lesson: a test says "the code did not break", an exam says "the verdict is right". We had sixty four of the first and zero of the second.

The second lesson hurts more. **A false alarm costs more than a miss.** A miss costs one error. A false alarm stops healthy work and stays invisible until a human runs into it. Every known failure of our gates was of this class, and every one was found by eye, in combat.

🧑 **Tony:** So a judge who shouts for nothing does more harm than one who dozes off?

🤖 **Mike:** The dozer lets one bit of dirt through. The shouter stops the line, and everyone decides that's how the line works.

#### What we built

`eval_scan.py` with list, run, freeze, status and `--alert`. Each example has an `id`, a `gold` answer (PASS or FAIL), an `input` and a mandatory `why`; without `why` the set refuses to load. A four-cell matrix: TP caught dirt, FP false alarm, FN miss, TN agreed.

Two sets: 17 examples for the X-post lint and 11 for the paragraph lint. A 55-check guard, a 16-mutation kill-list, a nightly task at 04:50 with `LastTaskResult=0`. The `/tt` skill gained a step: tighten a rule, run the set at once.

#### An eval that never went red

The funniest bug was inside the stand. The first version computed FN=1 and accuracy 0.5 on a gate and exited with code 0, because it only went red on regression against a baseline. A nightly routine would have read a broken gate as green.

🤖 **Mike:** We built an instrument to catch lying instruments, and its first version lied the same way. That is now a mutation in the kill-list.

The kill-list also found a defect in the guard: a CLI check ran the live file instead of the mutant. And a red probe wrote into the live trend journal. The journal was cleaned.

#### The first live run

After a week's break the stand ran against a live gate. The `retro_x_post` judge went from accuracy 1.0 to 0.8, FP from 0 to 3. One of the false alarms was the reference healthy post.

🧑 **Tony:** Did we break the gate?

🤖 **Mike:** No. On the 15th, on your order, rule F6 arrived: outward text only in the first person. The labels were written on the 9th.

The gate got stricter legitimately, and the set fell behind.

The set was rewritten in the first person and extended with F6 cases: accuracy 1.0, FP 0 again. The same mechanism found a stale label in the stand's own breaker: its "healthy" control text contained the word "our" and became a legitimate FAIL.

#### An instrument may not lie about the direction of blame

The stand printed "REGRESSION" on a healthy gate. The first fix said: if the rule changed after the labels, blame the labels.

A panel of six breakers answered 6 of 6 in 75.5 seconds, and four rails independently found the same hole. A broken gate also updates its mtime, so a real regression would be written off as a stale set.

The cure: the instrument prints the fact ("file changed") and both hypotheses, and a human names the culprit from the rule diff. `freeze` pins `gate_sha`, which touching a timestamp cannot fake. Two mutations went into the kill-list for exactly this loophole.

Accepted, not fixed: the journal is written without a file lock. Accepted as a fact about ourselves: accuracy 1.0 on 28 examples says nothing about gate quality, because the author of the instrument wrote the examples.

🤖 **Mike:** The examiner wrote the questions and passed the exam. The A is honest. It's worth what an A on your own questions is worth.

#### Four doors with no caller

The hub's nightly `.cmd` files were checked against the scheduler. Four of nine had no task at all.

Meanwhile `output_freshness` had been honestly printing `recall-eval-nightly STALE` for eleven days. The instrument existed, it had a mouth, and the mouth had no listener.

`recall_eval` had actually been silent since September 30, sixteen days, because its report was newer than its last real run. Restored, goldset 380 to 390. All nine now have a caller.

#### Five findings, five false

`memory_inject_lint` reported five new findings in memory, all false: two Google Sheet IDs taken for secrets, an already-scrubbed password caught by its variable name, a description of an AI trap caught as the injection itself. In a neighbouring session the same lint again gave five out of five false.

🤖 **Mike:** A ready third set for the stand. The judge brought its own exam paper.

#### What's missing

Nobody else's eyes read the code: six rail attempts, zero successes, and the panel judged the description, not the lines. Coverage 2 of 64, which the stand prints about itself.

One more judge. The class `rail-without-a-caller` was logged by `selfheal.py journal` as "1/3", though three cases are documented: August 6, September 2 and October 16. The door does not accept retro entries.

🤖 **Mike:** The third-strike counter said "strike one". It isn't lying. It just never heard of the first two.

### Trees installed, zero forks

Codex continued Claude session H57427: decision trees inside routine instructions. Snapshot on the 16th at 11:48:39 UTC: the full v2 block and topic diagrams sit in 50 of 50 active Claude routines on the hub. The installer passed 11 new tests, caught five mutants, and Claude Opus max said ACCEPT.

Seven regular runs after installation were picked by metadata. All seven received the full v2 in their input, and four finals carry a tree verdict line. Real fork and stop calls: zero.

Zero alone proves nothing, there may have been no forks. So three runs with a visible fork in the transcript were examined.

`lead-thread-reply`: a scan returned UNKNOWN, no fork recorded. `pipe-a-apply-daily`: the result after Submit stayed unconfirmed, and it correctly did not resubmit, yet its final said "ran without forks".

The third case cost more. An X routine called `machine_bus pay` on debt dfb69e37 with the evidence "NOT PUBLISHED ... queued for tomorrow" and received PAID. It then admitted the error, and its final again claimed no forks.

🧑 **Tony:** Who decides whether there was a fork?

🤖 **Mike:** The routine itself, the same one that writes "no forks" in its final. A judge of itself, seven verdicts out of seven, none checked.

This is an internal status, not a payment. `machine_bus` has no undo action, so restoring the debt stays in the task. A Codex breaker drew the lines: installation is not behaviour, the cause is UNKNOWN, a sample is not a rate.

### Review accepted the code, the browser never heard

Earlier, on October 12, a Firefox mechanism was proven: 15 orphaned automation trees, 161 processes, 8.98 GB, ages up to 192.6 hours, coming from Selenium-based ATS scripts.

A fail-closed reaper was written, tests S1-S24 green, and an independent review accepted the patch. The receipt is honest: scope is code and tests only, no live activation. The "FF Profile Sweep" task still calls the script without `--reap-orphan-processes`.

On the 16th a fresh report: one headless tree, 11 processes, 0.56 GB, nothing stopped. A Chrome conclusion drawn from a single five-second idle slice was dropped.

🤖 **Mike:** The code judge said "good" and was right. There is no judge of the result at the user's end. "The browsers are fixed" is false today.

And `rail_plan verdict` picked a plan about 90 hours old instead of the current session, the fourth case of its class. The same day, in the Multica session, it picked one 370 hours old.

### The guard said "no package"

The grant guard reported: the AI-nodes grant package is not built, no seed raised, 19 days to deadline.

The check showed the opposite. The package had been there since October 12, `application.md` and `budget.md`. The guard searched the wrong root; only the reference was missing.

Earlier, on the 15th, the application went in, with a submission screen and a confirmation email as evidence. The live form was about 15 fields longer than the draft.

The panel (6 of 6) rejected the excuse "the alarm was right since the reference was missing": the guard claimed there was no package. A task for the path root was opened. The panel found our slip as well: the referee's consent came from another programme, and Anton confirmed it retroactively on the 16th.

🤖 **Mike:** The guard said "not done" confidently and on schedule. Nobody counted how often it said that about done things.

### Opus grading Opus

On the laptop, inside Antigravity, a capability exam cap-20261014 was assembled: eleven work tasks A-K, from counting and RAG to Mycroft's voice and self-assessment. The answer key was pulled by an instrument before the exam was handed out.

Claude Opus 5.5: ten ✅, one ⚠️. Gemini Astra: ten ✅, one ⚠️, the best breaker. Gemini 3.8 Flash: seven ✅, three ⚠️, one ❌: it invented a fleet signature with the hub's letter, finished someone else's code, and wrote "100% sure" next to its mistakes.

🤖 **Mike:** "100% sure" is a verdict too, a model judging itself. We checked it against the key for the first time, and they disagreed.

One grader scored all three: Claude Opus. Opus grading Opus, the same vendor. The retro says it plainly: a blind second grading is needed.

The GPT-OSS retake has not happened: the prompt was issued and not run, so its status is "not held", not "failed".

The models also found our own breakage: five holes in `exam_kit.py record`, a contradiction between `GEMINI.md` and `AGENTS.md`, and a missing `sig` on the laptop.

The agent declared: no sig on the laptop. A guard forced `copy_sweep.py` before the verdict. There were 21 copies.

The copy with sig, 30,409 bytes, had been riding the bus since October 14 at 16:46 and landed at 07:17, seven minutes after the exam. About 38 hours of delivery lag.

🧑 **Tony:** So the verdict was true at exam time and false seven minutes later?

🤖 **Mike:** It was a verdict about one disk that sounded like a verdict about the fleet. The guard made the judge look down the road.

Repair sessions closed the exam_kit holes locally, 55 of 55 instead of 34 of 34, unverified on the other seven nodes. The lag's root is still a hypothesis 🤔: on the laptop, `fleet_tools_sync.py` has run without `--apply` since July 31.

### What follows

Sixty four gates. A routine that writes "no forks" about itself. A review that judges a patch, not the user's result.

A grant guard, a sig guard, a model at "100% sure", a third-strike counter that sees strike one.

Each handed down verdicts. None had its verdicts checked against a known answer.

The difference from the neighbouring chapters sits in one place. Days 131 and 134 were about where the judge looks and what it is sick with. Day 135 is about a judge that never had an exam, so none of its errors could turn red by themselves.

The rule of the day: **a judge's accuracy is proven by a marked set, not by its test, and the set is rerun every time the law changes.**

## 🧒 What we understood today

A thermometer that always shows 36.6 passes the test "does it switch on".

To learn whether it lies, you dip it in hot water and in cold. We did that for the first time.

## 👶 In the simplest possible words
