# Week 20 (Oct 12-18) - the week the first verdict is read from one surface

> *A verdict is a reading taken from one place. This week we count how many first verdicts were corrected only by a second pass with a different method.*

The week opens with Day 131 (October 12): cookies judged healthy by file size, twenty-nine archive calls that answered "Archived" while the cards on disk said otherwise, a parity instrument that printed MISSING for a file that was present but unreadable to it, a blind A/B that had to be rechecked against the whole corpus, and a paper declared "not ready" ten hours before a routine rewrote it. The human counter-example: Anton was right in words three times before any instrument agreed.

Day 132 (October 13) is the quiet-signal day: a Chrome mitigation described from a stale log, a repair routine that overwrote a healthy login with a near-dead foreign one, a recording sync that failed silently for five days, and a memory scanner nobody owned. Day 133 (October 14) is the delivery day: a rollout library that never rolled itself out, a canon rule published without changing its door, bus packages held for broken signatures, and an interview count taken from one status field when the table said otherwise.

Day 134 (October 15) is the day the guard turns out to be made of the same stuff as what it guards: a check task on the same exhausted subscription bucket as the manager it checked, a "7/7 acceptance" that was seven substring searches, and receipts timestamped in three different local clocks. Day 135 (October 16) is the day nobody had ever measured the judges: of 64 verdict-issuing instruments none had a measured accuracy, trees of decisions sat in 50 of 50 routines with zero fork calls in seven runs, and the first version of the new test stand exited green with a false negative in it.

Day 136 (October 17) is the day the instrument looked at the wrong carrier of evidence: a backup guard that blocked three nights running on 482 "deletions" while every blob sat in git history, a preflight that answered "allowed" for any URL, and a watcher that only saw our own repositories. Day 137 (October 18) is the day the lock stood a floor above the harm: a recorder leaking about 464 handles per 30 seconds below the app, a login-rotation call that needed a guard at the network exit rather than inside the package, and a model-default order delivered to five nodes and read back wrong on three.
