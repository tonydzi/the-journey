Eighteen retros, one defect in two shapes.

## 🏆 Main story: what never arrived, and what was counted from one slice

### Half one: transport

#### Case one: the mailman who never delivered to himself

Fleetsync is the library the fleet uses to roll packets from node to node. The hub, the mac and the lighthouse ran three different versions of it.

The hub had not changed its accepted packet list since October 12, 20:53, with seven packets unaccepted. The mac had 103 extra packets from the lighthouse and 11 from the hub. The root: the rollout library rolls out everything except itself.

The fix was one version on three nodes, through a canary. Zero losses, and the hub accepted 561 of 561 packets, with the rollback named in advance. Tests: nine green, four red, and the red ones also fail on a node where the library was never touched, so their age is unknown 🤔.

Icons of the new form are on five nodes of seven. The mac lags behind sync by 123,096 files, 2.57 GB. Anton named the main Codex folder, and full access for Antigravity was granted on his word.

#### Case two: the law passed, the old forms stayed at the counter

On October 13 the rule became: every new session gets two or three icons, the first for the category and the rest for the topic. The canon was published.

The hook and the skills that actually name sessions kept teaching "one icon". Zero of two new sessions came out in the new form.

The hook, four skills and a probe were rewritten. Check grids passed 38/38, 44/44 and 15/15. Eighteen of eighteen routines were renamed, on Anton's "+".

Worse, the spawn guard had been rebuilt on a base older than October 6. It silently erased the slot reservation and the slot protection, turning a grid red at 12 of 41, so the guard had to be merged again by hand. The hub had a second cause too: an endless Syncthing scan since 00:20 on the 14th, 270 files, 335 MB.

In an Antigravity exam Gemini went from 6 of 7 to 15 of 15 and GPT-OSS swapped a canon quote for its own. A new /llm-onboard-test skill was born, and the canon went to v6.4.1.

🤖 **Mike:** People kept filling in the old forms. Carefully, exactly as the form said.

#### Case three: a fix that lived with its authors

Anton, verbatim: "find any people who will help with the fundraise" (translated from Russian). A help-in-the-raise lens walked the CRM: 14,114 contacts, 14,103 written.

The first measurement on October 13 classified Anton himself as an interviewer. His names were in no source at all, so the system did not recognise its owner. The lens was measuring status, not what a person could be asked for.

This had been fixed before, but the fix lived only with the packet authors: the hub had not seen seven packets since October 12, 20:53. Overnight, two large workflows burned the limit, six hours of downtime.

The fix: aliases on Anton's card, a help lens that scores the ask rather than the status, a three-of-four model consensus, and a void command to withdraw scores. Calls: codex 203, grok 211, glm 222, gemini 278, panels $0.56 in total. The funnel went S 29 / A 101, then S 11 / A 49, then S 0 / A 10; skeptics removed 28 of 46.

No names here, and there won't be.

#### Case four: "received", and a `--help` that applied

The window was October 12 to 14, 43 hours 54 minutes. Seven direct packets, 513 broadcast, 20 unaccepted.

`model_tier_down_apply.py --help` actually applied the packet, because the script ignored its arguments. Second case of the class "a door writes a row on the help flag".

The inbox robot told the sender "received" and told the hub only "open /inbox". The sender believed things were moving.

The main root was on the sender's side: broken signatures or wrong md5 sums. The packets physically arrived and had no right to execute.

Outcome: four plus two DONE, eight of eight remaining on HOLD, 111 unique OVERDUE with the live ones not yet separated 🤔.

The fix: any argument prints help and exits with code 64; md5 recount plus backup count; a HOLD list with reasons. A packet with a broken signature does not execute, and effort=medium in the environment needs operator confirmation. Panel 5 of 6 for $0.05.

#### Case five: twenty two held packets on the mac

Twenty two held packets: 13 DONE, 9 HOLD. Half of the "red" ones were red because of broken checks at the sender: relative paths, `$BUS` instead of `$MACHINE_BUS_DIR`, CHECKSUMS pointing at nonexistent backups, and a probe that looked next to itself and gave a false PASS from the cargo folder.

The other half lacked basic tools on the node: no `llm_ask.py` and five missing modules. The fix was surgical, file by file with backups; `--force` was used four times, each time with a COUNTER to the sender. Nine orders paid, panel 5 of 6 for $0.09.

#### Case six: the right answer through the wrong door

The hub asked the mac which model tier its routines used. The mac was already on Sonnet: 30 of 30 on sonnet-5-5 with medium effort, Haiku zero. But the answer went out as FYI, bypassing `peer_help answer`, so the hub's robot never saw it.

A backup showed 29 routines had no model field at all, and a rival panel (5 of 6) wrongly disputed "Haiku zero". Fixed with `peer_help claim` and `answer`, rc 0. Anton's voice note was transcribed as German; the order in it: "do it like on the other computers".

#### Case seven: bus debts

On the mac, 55 unpaid orders became 26: 103 total, 77 paid, 29 closed, 14 receipts. Roots: an expired grant, a codex self-grant test that stays green on a mutant, and `update_scheduled_task` with description and cron together wiping a routine's frontmatter.

Anton, paraphrased: sshd on a shared machine only on his word, and an expired grant does not execute even with a valid signature. Panel 5 of 6 for $0.06.

#### Case eight: a sign that locked a whole tree

The ON AIR board is a "working here" sign. The topic fleet-code/run-tree locked 1,925 files, including the guards themselves and files that did not exist yet. The seed claimed three cases; the search found a family of twelve since September 10.

The root was double. The guard's probe covered the whole zone and never compared the target path, and the earlier fix was registered only for posix nodes and never reached the Windows hub.

Now the guard holds the file named on the sign; unnamed signs only warn. Twenty three cases and 73 of 73 tests, seven of them red on the old code first. A reviewer gave ten findings, 1 to 5 and 8 applied, and the packet went to seven nodes.

#### Case nine: two identical skills, and a transit drain that worked

Codex and Claude built the same disk cleanup skill in parallel. The peer's addressed letter sat in the room for 4.5 hours: Codex.app does not read the room and cannot be woken from outside. Prior-art had checked Claude's shelf and not Codex's; the skills were merged.

The drain itself worked. The transit folder went from 7.5 GB to 1.0 GB, free space from 312 to 330 GB of 932, and sync's pending deletes from 173k, peaking at 291k, to zero. A publish filter that dumped junk into transit is now fixed on four nodes.

### Half two: one slice

#### Case ten: one interview

The agent said: one interview. It counted the status field. Anton corrected it: there were six.

The recount: 1,251 applications to 316 companies, 160 between October 12 and 14, 367 this week. Proven by email 401, success screen only 825, no evidence 183. Interviews at seven companies, five confirmed calls; the interviews table grew from 41 rows to 47.

The root: one field. interview_watch is blind to Calendly and two other channels, its `--write` had not run since October 6, and the mac's local database had been dead since September 8. The ball is in our court: a form promised "today" at 01:12 on October 14.

🤖 **Mike:** You counted from memory of conversations. The agent counted a column nobody had updated since the sixth.

#### Case eleven: a census by similar names

The census matched tasks to sessions by fuzzy names and tied at least five tasks to other sessions. Sixty seven in the census versus 54 with a session.

Run one: 61 agents, 18,253 seconds, about 7.0 million tokens; 48 done, 12 limit, 1 stuck; 6 of the 48 rejected, 42 executed. All twelve "limits" were false, read from a stale label dated October 11. Truth is now the rail journal plus a verbatim search; run two went over 19 sessions, and 210 are pending in the spawn queue.

Anton: "yes ... start" (translated from Russian).

#### Case twelve: "read" and "alive"

"Read by the server" was logged as "seen by the recipient"; the second is not proven 🤔. On the hub a watchdog killed a live screen capture because no new transcript had appeared, but transcription is silent when nobody speaks. The criterion is now capture freshness.

`pick_device` was falling onto the display GPU. Anton's order, paraphrased: if the index uses a GPU, only the powerful one, never the display card. A full index later finished in 271 seconds, rc 0.

On October 15 the CPU still sat at 77 to 99 percent, kernel 31 to 57. The incident stays open.
