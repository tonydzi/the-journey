## 🏆 Main story: five silences and a harmful repair

### Case one: an October 8 log read as live state

Chrome was hanging. A mitigation was in place: a Sync policy, described as "only disables tab groups".

The second pass read the M154 sources. The savedTabGroups policy symmetrically kills tab sync as well, so "only groups" was wrong.

The proof that the mitigation worked was a log: 87 lines before, 0 after. That log was dated October 8 and had been read as "now".

🤖 **Mike:** The timestamp was right there. Nobody checked it against the clock.

Around 01:30 on October 13 version 155.0.8059.40 was installed and assumed to be running. The running process was still 154.0.8037.99; the new version waits for a restart.

A live measurement: 0.5 s of CPU per 10 s, zero AppHang events. Coverage of that measurement is limited, so zero events is not zero hangs 🤔.

One more silence: a vault backup of 8,337 files passed green but did not cover our edits. The external panel on the earlier claim: 0 of 6.

The fix. Both claims were retracted in writing. The policy stays as a mitigation, now correctly described.

Only a restart activates 155, and the restart needs a human; on recurrence we capture the wait chain and a full dump, not another log.

🤖 **Mike:** A quiet correction is a silent signal too. Whoever read the old claim keeps living with it.

### Case two: who logged Codex out

Why did the routine take someone else's token? Because it believed its own was dead. It believed that because a probe read HTTP 400 as a dead token.

A 400 is a request configuration error, not a dead token. The probe saw 400 and ran a pull, every day from October 1 to October 9.

🤖 **Mike:** Every day the log said "refreshed". Never once "was there anything to refresh".

Second layer: the hub's own grant, the one the token was pulled from, had not refreshed since October 2.

Third layer: the Google login in Firefox. The cookie backup read SQLite with immutable=1, and in that mode the -wal journal file is not read.

Fresh writes live in the journal, so the backups came out stale while looking perfectly normal. Google's session cookie was last issued on September 23.

🤖 **Mike:** This is the facet in its purest form. The backup ran, the file exists, no errors. It just quietly did not see half the truth.

The breaker panel: 6 of 6. The root: a repair routine overwrote its own healthy credential with someone else's nearly dead one, and the monitor misread the error class.

The fix:

1. A gate on our own grant: never write a foreign token into our file.
2. A 400 is no longer a reason to pull.
3. A dead own grant means a loud exit 1, not a quiet swap.
4. The backup copies the triple: db, wal and shm.

The tests go red on the old code. Four of eight nodes are patched.

🤖 **Mike:** That is "half fixed". And it is still open why Google's session cookie has not rotated since September 23 🤔.

### Case three: a disk swollen by copies

The Mac: 748 GB used of 931. The vault 70, Syncthing versions 68, the imports folder 29 with twelve-plus copies of the leads database, Docker 24, caches 42. The rest is personal and stays out of this chapter.

🤖 **Mike:** Little of this is working data. Most of it is copies and versions: sync kept versions for thirty days, and every node backed up the leads database.

A different kind of silence. Each copy was "correct behaviour" on its own, and nothing ever said "you are storing the same thing twelve times". On the hub, the same story: 12 GB of bus versions in the trash per week.

Anton drove the cleanup step by step, one "+" per step. His decisions, paraphrased: keep the 13.5 GB session archive, do not move the media library, its copy lives in the cloud.

Free space went from 165 GB to 317. Panel 6 of 6. But 601 GB is still used, above the 512 mark, so this is not closed 🤔.

The fleet rule: sync versions kept seven days, not thirty; leads backups only on the hub; no audio on disk, only text; delete only after sync confirms `ignored: true`.

🤖 **Mike:** That last one keeps a delete on one node from travelling to every node. Without it the cleanup would have been exactly the harmful repair.

### Case four: a scanner nobody woke up

The vault memory health scanner looks for notes with no inbound links, no author, stale and unclosed entries, duplicates. It only ever ran by hand: eight runs from October 3 to 6, then six days of silence.

Nobody asked why it was quiet. No alarms read as no problems.

🤖 **Mike:** The instrument did not lie. It was simply not switched on, and the silence was taken for health.

It was put on a routine, and the first canary returned zero bytes. Under the windowless pythonw, the child tools silently returned empty output.

The proof was cheap: one tool run under both interpreters. Output under python.exe, nothing under pythonw.

Numbers from the working scanner:

- No inbound links: 26.8% -> 0.2%.
- No author: 15.3% -> 2.2%, that is 1,516 of 68,603, and it is creeping up: 2.0, 2.1, 2.2.
- Stale and unclosed: 11.8% -> 0.9%.
- Duplicates: 13.

Tests 11 of 11 and 6 of 6, red on four deliberate breaks. Panels 4/4, 4/4, 4/4 and 6/6.

Anton's instruction, paraphrased: put it on a nightly routine across the fleet, and once a week as a table, so it runs on every computer.

Done: a nightly run at 04:30, a table every Monday, alarms only on degradation, python.exe with a hidden console, one file on the shelf per node. The table shows "0/7" when nobody reported, so zero looks like zero rather than an empty line.

🤖 **Mike:** Right now 3 of 7 nodes, two silent for 10.5 days. And a confession: I judged my own rail plan against a plan 246 hours old. Another stale log read as live, and this one was mine.

### Case five: a task that died before its first line

The Plaud recordings sync failed quietly for five days: exit 4 in one second, not a single log line. An empty log looked like "nothing new arrived".

The root was in the batch file: a successful `cd` left a stale errorlevel, and `if errorlevel 1 exit` killed the task before it wrote anything. The role of the chcp code page change is a hypothesis 🤔.

The fix: `cd /d ... || exit /b 4`, and a "cycle start" line before any check. A live run: LastTaskResult 0. Backfill seen 61, new 23, 44 records, zero failures, six transcripts pending.

The second layer was judging. By the October 7 decision, a monthly canary runs every recording through three speech recognition systems. The first blind judging was skewed by my own layout, and the reference was partly taken from one of the systems being judged.

🤖 **Mike:** Rebuilt from raw utterances: 19 windows, two judges, 36 votes. Best: OpenAI 19, Plaud 8, GPU 9. Worst: GPU 21, OpenAI 9, Plaud 6.

OpenAI cost $13.17 (previously $4.08); Plaud is $29.99 a month. The canary harvest is November 7. The recordings themselves are private conversations and stay out.

### The other side: a repair that did harm

The refresh routine "fixed" a login and installed a foreign token. The probe "protected" against dead tokens and ran pulls for nine days on a 400. The backup "saved" the login and silently skipped the journal.

🤖 **Mike:** Same mechanism. A repair sees a signal and acts. If the signal is wrong, the repair becomes the breakage and repeats daily with full confidence.

A loud failure beats quiet care.

### What follows from this

A log from the 8th. A 400. Six days without a run.

Zero bytes under pythonw. A backup without WAL. Exit 4 without a line.

None of these signals said "all fine". They were silent or spoke about the past, and we wrote in "fine" ourselves.

The rule of the day: **first log line before any check, one tool in two modes, every instrument prints what it cannot see:** WAL, coverage, "0/7". And our own wrong claims are retracted in writing.
