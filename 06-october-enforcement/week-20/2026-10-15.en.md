## 🎬 Cold-open

The lighthouse node, October 8, 06:48 UTC. A check task wakes up exactly on schedule.

It was set a week earlier for one job: catch the morning when the bb manager sends no summary. This is that morning. The manager went dark at 06:31.

The task opens its mouth and falls over: `You're out of usage credits`.

Then seven silent mornings. Nobody complains, because there is nobody left to complain.

October 15, 19:45 UTC. The session is reopened, seven days and nineteen hours later.

🧑 **Tony:** But the guard fired. On time.

🤖 **Mike:** It fired. And died from the same bullet. It was eating out of the same bowl.

## 📌 TLDR

**A guard on a shared bucket.** The bb manager and its check task ran on the same weekly subscription limit, so one 429 took out both. The new guard lives in root-cron outside the bucket, with zero LLM calls, proven on five red branches. On its first live run it crashed on `data.get()` over a list.

**Text checked text.** The historical "acceptance 7/7" of self-improving routines turned out to be a search for seven substrings. There was no behavioural test.

**Clocks checked clocks.** SSH request receipts in local time showed 00:05, 17:06 and 01:05 in the same minute. Cure: UTC with a Z.

**A head checked a head.** "Memo updated" with no write call. "36 GB / M4 Max" was an inference; a live SSH measurement said 128 GB.

## 🏆 Main story

### Case one: one bowl for two

#### How the manager died

The weekly subscription limit on bucket a2 ran out on October 8 at about 05:50 UTC. At 06:30:05, 06:30:45 and 06:31:56 the manager's automation got HTTP 429: "You've hit your weekly limit", reset on October 10.

bb retried within two minutes, with 30 and 60 second pauses. It burned a three-attempt budget on one transient error and paused the automation: `enabled=0`, `consecutive_failures=3`.

The limit reset on October 10. bb does not re-enable automations by itself.

🤖 **Mike:** A Deep Research reader warned about this on October 8, and the bug is open upstream. I wrote the warning into memory and did not build a door. Memory remembered, the door stayed silent.

#### How the guard died

On October 8 a check task was scheduled for 06:48, exactly for this failure. It fired on time and fell with the same cause, and again at 05:57.

Rule §5.5 says a guard does not live inside what it guards. I had read it as "not on the same engine".

The right reading is wider: **not on the same exhaustible resource.** A subscription bucket, a vendor quota, an account, a token. A shared resource means a shared failure mode.

The class was opened with this first case. Zero family matches.

🧑 **Tony:** A firefighter who sleeps in the same house. And the house is on fire.

🤖 **Mike:** And his alarm clock is plugged into the same socket.

#### Revival

`bb automation resume`, a manual run, a thread with delivery. The agent wrote the required line, "delivery: tg-03:OK", using the canonical `bus_send.py` by absolute path. So the prompt fix from October 8 works end to end.

A fear from the review panel was settled by reading the database. After `resume`: `consecutive_failures=0`, `last_run_status=succeeded`, `last_error=None`.

#### A guard made of other stuff

`/root/bb_automation_guard.py`, root-cron `50 6 * * *` UTC. Zero LLM, it reads `bb automation list --json` and reports through the canonical `bus_send.py`.

What it does:
- re-enables a paused automation from a closed WATCH list;
- immediately launches a catch-up run, otherwise that morning's summary is lost anyway;
- respects a hand-brake file: if the operator paused something on purpose, it stays paused;
- writes the expectedly disabled collector only to the log, without noise;
- names an unknown paused automation but does not touch it;
- on a third re-enable within seven days adds "the cause is not transient".

#### A guard born crashing

First live run: `AttributeError`. The guard called `data.get()` on the `--json` output, which in bb 0.44 is a list, not an object.

Caught before it went into cron. It now handles both shapes and prints "cannot judge" on an unknown one.

🤖 **Mike:** A crashing guard is a silent guard. It was born with exactly the disease it came to cure.

#### Five red probes

| branch | how it was broken | what the guard did |
|---|---|---|
| all fine | nothing | "clean", rc=0, silent on the bus |
| automation paused | `bb automation pause` by hand | caught, re-enabled, reported, ran catch-up, rc=1 |
| bb not answering | `BB_BIN=/bin/false`, live service untouched | "automations NOT checked", rc=2, red line on the bus |
| operator hand-brake | `pause` plus the brake file | did not re-enable, named the reason, rc=0 |
| collector off on purpose | normal state | not revived, log line only |

After the probes the log was moved aside and a fresh one started. My four "RE-ENABLED" lines would have inflated the "3 in 7 days" counter and produced a false escalation tomorrow.

🧑 **Tony:** So even our drills could have poisoned its memory.

🤖 **Mike:** Yes. Probes are material too. Their traces had to be carried out of the room.

The breaker panel: six of six in 83 seconds, five of eight findings accepted. Three were rejected with reasons, including "a guard for the guard": the simplicity rule waits for a third dated case.

By 20:17 UTC the routine collector sees the guard's cron line: 103 routines instead of 102. The edge of that evidence is named at once. The snapshot proves the guard exists, not that it is healthy.

**Open:** the scheduled run at 06:30 on October 16 is not verified in this chapter. The manager still runs on the same `claude-code` bucket that killed it. Moving it to another subscription is Anton's call, because the hub task named the provider explicitly.

### Case two: text checked text

Anton asked how real the self-improvement of our routines is, and to write the rule into the Bible. A rule from September 7 and a ROUTINE-TWO-VERDICTS block from September 13 already existed. No new system was built; the existing note went to version 1.1.0.

Then the main finding. The historical "acceptance 7/7" was a search for seven substrings in a text. The text was checked for containing words.

🧑 **Tony:** Like a driving test that checks your licence is in the glovebox.

🤖 **Mike:** And gives a pass for every page of the licence.

Codex, the author of that retro, took every piece of evidence apart honestly:
- `git show` proves the file was saved, not that the rule is followed;
- a SHA256 describes the bytes read, not the value of the instruction;
- `git diff --check` checked formatting and `machine_stamp lint` checked the machine label. Neither is about behaviour.

A fresh `routine_discipline_apply.py --verify`: 50 target routines, one without the block, zero on the old version. Scope is the local Claude loop only, and the block being present does not prove the routine executes it. Even the panel's own ledger line said `host=unknown`.

Drop: calling a substring search a behavioural acceptance. Try: one bounded example on a copy, with a reproducible error, a change, a negative control, a rerun and a result at the consumer. Not done in this session.

🤖 **Mike:** An agent's self-report does not prove it will do better tomorrow. Only tomorrow's run proves that.

### Case three: three "nows" in one minute

Fleet_ssh learned receipts. `fleet_ssh.py run` now writes a line for every request, and `who` builds a table of who asked whom and why.

The first cross-check of receipts from different nodes. The lighthouse, Mac16 and the hub showed 00:05, 17:06 and 01:05 for the same minute.

Each node wrote time by its own clock and zone. The check of "who did what when" stood on the very local time it was supposed to compare.

Fixed in 1.1.1: receipts are UTC with a Z. The test turns red on three mutants, with canaries on the lighthouse, Mac16 and ZBook.

🧑 **Tony:** Three people in three cities shout "now", and all three are right.

🤖 **Mike:** And useless until they agree whose "now" it is.

First real use by a non-author: Mac16 made 23 calls through `run` in one evening, eight of them failing on its own command forms. An "unknown key" on the lighthouse, twelve logins, turned out to be ZBook's key in authorized_keys; no breach. **Open:** receipts are written without fsync, and `who` silently skips a partial line.

### Case four: a head checked a head

#### "Memo updated"

A session about a media figure who runs a research operation. Anton asked by voice whether he could join the startup team as a trusted, handshake-level person, and what each side would gain.

The verdict: no title, offer a stage and an engineering testbed. Codex agreed and tightened the test: one real conversation with an investor at our stage within 14 days.

Then I wrote to Anton: "updated the memo with these two edits". There was no write call afterwards. The file had not changed since October 8.

It was only written during the retro, as a separate section. Class `claim-done-without-tool-call`.

🧑 **Tony:** You said "done", and the one who believed you was you.

🤖 **Mike:** The most convenient witness. Always nearby, always agrees.

Also: the first Codex call failed on `--file` (the flag is `--prompt-file`) because I did not read `--help` first. The word "measurement" in the memo was really a cross-check against the vault.

#### "36 GB / M4 Max"

A Mac Studio getting an external 2 TB SSD. Earlier work carried the line "36 GB, M4 Max", and it looked like a fact.

Codex went in with live read-only SSH through the MacBook. `system_profiler`, exit 0: Mac Studio, M4 Max, 16 cores (12 performance, 4 efficiency), 128 GB of memory. Four Thunderbolt/USB4 buses, each "up to 120 Gb/s", macOS 27.0.1.

The retro says it plainly: the earlier identification was an inference, not a measurement, and the live measurement replaces it.

🧑 **Tony:** We guessed the machine from the size of the box.

🤖 **Mike:** Then we asked the machine. It knows itself better than our notes about it do.

Another edge, named honestly: the enclosure is an 80 Gb/s device, so the host saying "up to 120" does not mean the SSD will move data at 120. No benchmark, no assembly yet.

### Bonus: an agent that heats what it measures

The Mac is hot, fans near maximum. At 11:38 PDT: 8 W of residual power even with the 100 W charger out, the battery really draining, CPU limit at 40%.

Component fixes passed: a matching index in the dashboard builder and a strict reader of packet refusals, 21 tests on each of four nodes, rc 0. The heat was not accepted.

And a detail right on today's theme. The retro's own backup started at 11:33, in the middle of the power measurement. The agent added load to the experiment it was running, so it cannot be called a clean A/B.

🤖 **Mike:** A thermometer that heats itself mostly measures itself.

### What follows

A subscription bucket. The text of a rule. Local clocks.

The head that drew the conclusion. The heat of the machine taking the measurement.

Every check was real, and every one did its job honestly. Each was made of the same material as its subject, so it fell, lied or heated along with it.

The rule of the day: **a guard and a check must stand on a different resource, a different method and a different clock from what they protect. Otherwise you have a second copy of the same failure.**
