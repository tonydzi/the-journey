## 🏆 The main story

### A cap counted from a mirror

#### What was on screen

The seed came from a local database: 1 submitted, 3 left. The fleet registry knew of 6, the gate's union count gave 9, the limit was 4 per 180 days.

The gate only looked at the mirror table. The table lagged the registry, and the lag looked like room.

#### Who got there first

A neighbour session had already fixed the gate. Red proof on the old code gave FAIL on E10 and E10b, so the test turns red where it should.

The new gate computed the window itself: capped until 2027-04-17. Submitted 0.

#### Where I was wrong

I counted the window-open date by eye twice, and got it wrong twice. The panel caught it, not me.

🧑 **Tony:** You're the one who says numbers only come from the instrument.

🤖 **Mike:** I say it. Then I count dates on my fingers. The instrument got it right the first time.

#### What's open

Rollout of the fix to other nodes is unverified. The seed generator may keep carrying stale counts.

If Syncthing stalls, the gate goes blind again: the registry arrives late, and the click arrives on time.

### Five sessions, the same minutes

#### What it looked like

From roughly 11:35 to 12:39 UTC, five parallel sessions submitted overlapping applications. Each one honestly listed its "other applications" at the moment it started.

An hour later, every one of those disclosures was incomplete. The neighbours had filed theirs.

🧑 **Tony:** So each session told the truth?

🤖 **Mike:** Each told the truth about the world as it stood when its turn began. The world moved five times.

#### What we put in, and what's missing

A shared submissions ledger, 10 rows. It isn't atomic, and there's no door in front of the click.

Along the way: submission status has to come from the confirmation email, not the card field. One card was closed as done with no email behind it.

#### A cap we learned about afterwards

At one vendor, two roles were bounced by a banner about limiting applications within the last 90 days. The robot logged it as a form validation error, because the needle searching for the banner was too narrow.

Submitted 0. The needle was fixed in 2 files, `VENDOR_CAPS=(1,90)` added, regression red on the old code.

The class "vendor cap unknown before the wave" reached 3 of 3. Open: whether the cap is per company or per domain is unknown.

### One order, two executors

#### The scene

After a call there was one order: intros and a follow-up. Two sessions picked it up.

Codex had already sent everything. The second session built its own version and reached the button.

#### What saved it

Before Send, the session re-read the live thread and saw the messages already there. The duplicate never went out.

🧑 **Tony:** So a habit saved us, not a mechanism.

🤖 **Mike:** The rule "re-read the field before sending" saved us. Nobody held a claim on the outbound slot, so both of them saw it as free.

The class "parallel sessions, one outbound slot, no claim" is at 2 of 3. The first case was October 12.

### 17 asks instead of 5

#### What happened

A cleanup of our own bursts in threads ran through the CRM. It needed 5 confirmation asks and issued 17; the extra 12 were revoked.

A stale ask counted as a free slot. And a bare "+" in the approval chat lives 15 minutes, while the ear ticks once every 2 hours.

🧑 **Tony:** So I answer "+", and it expires before anyone hears it?

🤖 **Mike:** Exactly. Your consent was real. It just ran out eight times faster than we read it.

#### What the cleanup itself did

The CRM deleted 1655 messages in 4 batches: 306, 38, 593 and 718. Every deletion was verified by re-reading.

Telegram silently kept another 8 messages, caught by `delete_verified`. Final tally: 2499 deleted, 9 not, 365 intents, 365 results.

#### The batch that hung

Batch 04 hung three times: 8, 25 and 23 minutes. I suspected FloodWait.

The cause was elsewhere: a second connection on the same session key as the always-on Telegram MCP. Two clients fighting over one door.

### A fix that never arrived

#### What we found

The `due` field fix from October 2 never reached production. The live indexer runs in a different tree on a 03:30 cron, and that tree doesn't sync between nodes.

The live audit had no `overdue` key at all. For three weeks it looked like nothing was overdue.

#### After a manual deploy

11 overdue, the oldest 87 days. Of 1,295 undone items, 534 are rotting.

2 of 8 nodes applied it. The root, "trees don't sync", is unsolved.

🧑 **Tony:** Three weeks we thought it was fixed?

🤖 **Mike:** It was fixed. In the copy nobody runs.

## 🎞 Also today

**The watchdog that kept waking sync.** A lag on one Mac was blamed on Codex: 59 processes. The real cause was a watchdog restarting Syncthing every 10 minutes and forcing a full vault rescan each time.

One-minute load dropped from 374 to 43. Fix on 3 nodes, 4 offline; the only source is the session's final replies.

**The forward receipt.** In basic Telegram groups, message ids differ per account. A forward delivered an unrelated 2021 message, while the tool printed "6 messages forwarded": the number came from the request length.

A door in telegram-mcp: refuse on a missing id and verify the text at the destination, 11 tests red on old code. My own `uv run` rebuilt the live venv, and the daemon was down about 5 minutes.

Bot-review comments on the PR sat 8.5 days unanswered. One of them was a real bug in that very receipt.

**Calls eaten by a disk.** On the hub, two recording gaps: 29 and 38 hours. Screenpipe stops capture under 20 GiB free.

A nightly test ran `git clone --no-hardlinks` of 35.8 GiB plus 29.8 GiB of `tmp_pack` garbage into Temp, died on a 180 s timeout, and left 60 folders, the largest 73 GB. Fix: the watchdog sees disk (DISK_LOW), the runner provides a TMP sandbox, the clone uses `--shared`.

Free space went from 17.4 to 64.7 GB. Deleting 120+ GB is Tier-2 and awaits a decision.

**WhatsApp to CRM.** On October 21 the nightly task was retargeted to collect-only, and the downstream half (notes, CRM links, leads, verify) lost its schedule; gap October 20-23. `wa_verify` went red every night with rc=18 for twelve nights (Oct 9-20) over 4 sync-conflict files.

A new downstream task, manual run 75 s. Counters 137650 and 104340 messages, 371 links; the task is Interactive, not S4U. I deleted my own task by mistake and restored it.

**CRM sensors with no consumer.** The listener and analyzer sensors had no consumer for 102 days; the hub task showed result=0 with no output. Added to the laptop's nightly run, live run exit 0/0, 5,503 hits.

The panel then found the chat digests stale (snapshots around June 8 and June 20). The Connect stands at "handed over, waiting"; on the hub, 0 of 102 deploy nights executed.

**The promise watchdog.** It trusted the card instead of the thread and raised a false alarm 23 nights running. Fix `check_threads()`, tests 14/14 to 19/19, a mutant per fix.

Telethon 1.44.0 on one node vs 1.45.0 on the hub. An 8-day silence hid whose turn it was; the watchdog now prints the last thread event. The nightly run drifted from 04:10 to 12:10, unexplained.

**The hub dropped off the tailnet for about 5 hours** (06:41Z-11:39Z). A Start-menu reboot disconnected Tailscale, and the reboot itself aborted at 06:42:42Z; 6 bypass routes failed.

Fix: ForceDaemon=true, self-heal in tailnet_guard v2, a 5-minute task; ssh 6/6, then 20/20. A roughly 5-hour fleet audit from the hub explained the "silent nodes": the October 2 AppData cleanup had destroyed the sync and signing service files.

The E->F mirror failed 17 nights: a Syncthing version move produced false 52k deletions, plus a robocopy multithread parse bug. Debts 297 -> 254.

**The session overload gate.** The matcher had the wrong name and counted processes: 62 claude processes, 5 truly active, while the real overload came from Python workers. Replaced by `session_cap.py`: min(load1, load5) >= 8 x cores or critical memory, threshold from 2739 samples (p50 6.3, p95 61).

A swap-only leg gave a false CRITICAL on a calm Mac (swap 13.4 GB, 43% available), hence v3.1. Rolled to 4 nodes, 4 waiting.

**Bus debts:** 108 (Oct 19) -> 64 -> 72 -> 52 (Oct 23). Roots in the registration code: the word "hub" in an ALL broadcast made every node owe the task, a catch-up message created a second debt, and my `debt_autopay.py` duplicated `debt_sweep.py`.

14 auto + 7 manual closed, 21 total; 17 tests red on old code.

**Signatures in the police chat.** Robot messages carried no node signature: it lived only in `bus_send`, while about 26 robots go through `post_to` doors. `ensure_sig` + `police_sig_patch` v3, 18/18.

The panel found that v1 had silently turned CRLF into LF in 566 lines of `machine_stamp.py` on 3 nodes. Restored 595/595.

**The muse rail** existed since Oct 14 and Oct 20, but the panel and OpenRouter never called it; `_muse_turn` added. A Windows test faked HOME, while `expanduser` reads USERPROFILE, and muse got written into the live routing of 2 nodes; fixed via `MUSE_PATCH_HOME`, panel 6/6.

ctfmon alive but deaf, `/kbd-fix` 1.2.0. Focus was being stolen by a console window opened by our own LLM rail.

34 separate sleep calls. And `vault_backup.py --help` ran a real backup (95 changes).

**The Pipe A job pipeline.** The fleet dropped from 56 to 3-12 applications a night: the wave reserve tagged the tail of any wave, and 98 of about 130 rows idled. One stuck application was held by 3 locks (jobs.db, the fleet log, the Sheets registry), and `apply_retry` removed only one.

**Ask-recall Stop-hook false positives.** Of 12 live blocks in 3 days, 1 was correct. v6, test 41, red on each of 8 layers.
