## 🏆 Main story

### "Two papers"

#### What happened

The speech draft said "two". The source has three papers with three different DOIs.

The number came from a previous session's context, not from the source. It is the fifth case in this family.

#### Why the door stayed silent

`outbound_fact_lint.py` checks numbers against the source. Its threshold: two digits and more.

Numbers in words and single digits aren't in its definition. "Two", "three", "5" slip past, though small counts are exactly the ones about people, papers and submissions.

🧑 **Tony:** And the second instrument, the one that looks for what we already have?

🤖 **Mike:** `have.py` searches by file name. It missed a copy of the master under another name, because to it that's a different file.

#### As many instruments, as many answers

On one family two instruments diverged: 4 against 288. My own surface counts were off too, 28 instead of 22.

None of these numbers was invented. Each one honestly answered its own definition of a "case".

#### What's open

A change to the fact door's threshold is not confirmed in the digest. Neither is search by content instead of name.

### A limit of 4 that held 5

#### What broke outside

The employer switched off its separate jobs host and embedded the form into its own site. The robot judged whether a vacancy was alive from the page text and closed 21 live rows.

After the review, 22 were reopened.

#### What broke inside

The limit per employer was 4. In fact there were 5 submissions.

One application with a submit date later got status closed. The counter only counted open rows, and that one dropped out of the count.

🧑 **Tony:** So the application went out, and the limit forgot about it?

🤖 **Mike:** The limit never knew it. In its definition "submission" meant "open row", and a closed one with a submit date was nothing to it.

#### What we fixed

10 fixes. The truth about a vacancy's life now comes from the platform API, not from the page text.

`_sent_pair` counts closed with a submit date as a submission.

### A limit banner disguised as a field error

#### What happened

The platform showed a banner: two applications per role in 2 months. The classifier filed it as a field error.

The remaining headroom under the cap was 1, not 3. The nightly pipe hit one employer for about 9 nights, October 14 to 23.

#### Root

`classify_ashby_outcome` checked the field error before the page text. The first match won, and the limit check never got its turn.

1071 descriptions lack the limit needle. Check order is part of the definition too: whatever the instrument saw first is what it named.

🧑 **Tony:** Nine nights in a row we knocked on a closed door and thought we'd filled the form wrong?

🤖 **Mike:** Yes. And every night the instrument was right about the field and blind to the banner.

#### What we put in

The limit is caught before field errors. Stops with an expiry, auto-close at 60 days.

#### What's missing

The auto-stop is proven only by a test on a temporary journal. The digest shows no live trigger.

### 50 sessions prepared, 0 continued

#### What happened

The task: resume 50 app sessions. Out of 14478 files, 500 fresh ones were picked, 258 of them candidates, and a list of 50 is ready.

Continuations sent: 0.

#### Where the definition got swapped

Anton answered: "Yes, continue these 50." His yes is permission, not proof of launch.

The "done" counter measured preparation. The question was about continuation.

🧑 **Tony:** Did the panel catch it?

🤖 **Mike:** Panel 6 of 6: five groups of remarks accepted, three rejected. Why the sessions closed in the first place is still not established.

### What follows

The fact door, the submission limit, the classifier, the "done" counter. None of them lied within its own bounds.

Rule of the day: **before trusting a counter, read its definition and find your case inside it; if your case isn't in the definition, the instrument has no answer to your question.**

## 🎞 What else happened today

**The freshness field and the silence watchdog.** One lab's script turned out to be an orphan: nobody called it. Another lab's robots.txt forbids automated collection, so we go through an aggregator.

The freshness field is written by the producer itself, so it is worthless as evidence. The watchdog checks 75 labs: no fresh row for more than 7 days. The principle went down as "a watchdog belongs on the output, not on the launch".

The panel found three holes: zero margin on the threshold, a bus path without a test, a fresh date on a truncated response.

**Voice001 root delta.** The request went to 18 recipients; the priority one was confirmed by reading at 10, recheck 10 of 10. The retro draft credited the root with a forward it never made; a 6 of 6 panel caught it.

Authorship was checked from a retelling, not from the session's calls. In the routines snapshot the blind zone was 367 of 786, and 84 routines were failing.

**STOP_DUP direct letters.** The source hid a sender error behind a total of 0. The description "guard is checked" didn't distinguish one check per call from a recheck between batches.

Fixed: CV path from config, an error no longer turns into 0, STOP before work. 18 tests, 2 red controls, today's sends 0.

**False dead application links.** Of 173 rows, 128 live, 45 real corpses. Greenhouse wrappers outside the manual map were counted as dead.

"Which/what…" questions were swallowed by the yes/no rule, the 4th case of the class. The 4/180 limit was broken by an inter-node race: a total of 5 against a limit of 4.

**Fleet audit.** Claude.ai sync returned exit 0 after a failed pull. 94 future dates and 20 failures from CIM were refuted by direct COM; 9503 potential mirror deletions do not equal lost files.

**prior_art shards.** In a day three nodes added 3 families, one more node 1; families 78 → 82. 14 parcels were in flight with different installer copies, node data rode inside the code file, and the installer overwrote the node's file.

Fixed: node shards, an add-family door, TABLE-KEEP doesn't overwrite the node's file. v7 is on one node only, three nodes unchecked.

**Screenpipe and export.** The export took the parent's ID instead of the helper's own UUID, and night processing overwrote the parent chat: 380 instead of 1236 turns, 231 instead of 1494. The "zero" in search was an HTTP 403 with a 24-hour limit, not a loss; writers brought secrets back after a one-time cleanup.
