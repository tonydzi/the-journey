## 🏆 The main story

### A guard silent for six weeks

#### Why the silence was false

The hook checks that the home folder belongs to this machine. The config it compares against disappeared.

The code met that with `except BaseException: pass` and exited quietly. From outside, the guard's silence and the guard's agreement looked the same.

🤖 **Mike:** The first guess was the registry, which knows 2 nodes out of 8. The registry is incomplete and honestly silent about six of them. The guard was silent while pretending to know.

The root was named: an instrument blind with no signal of its blindness.

#### What was built and how it was proven

"CANNOT JUDGE" now stands on all five paths where the guard used to go blind quietly. The config rides next to the hook, so the two can no longer get lost one at a time.

`_test_home_dir_guard.py` went 15 of 15, then 18 of 18. The old version is red: 4 of 15. After the panel the count stood at 16 of 18.

External review ran two rounds, 4 findings and one more, all closed. Panel 6 of 6, $0.091, all six COUNTER.

#### A near miss

The agent first checked file freshness with `ls | tail`, and `ls` sorts by name. A second measurement with `ls -dt` corrected the conclusion.

#### What is missing

The `--check` canary passed; a node of a different type is not proven. Five nodes outside the registry stay silent by design, and a separate session was raised for them. The `llm_ask` wrapper lost the answer twice and was bypassed with a direct `codex exec`.

### Toggle on, login off

The goal was to drive sessions from the phone through Remote Control. State read `remoteControlState: on`, and `claude rc` failed with "You must be logged in".

The app and the CLI have separate logins. `claude auth status` returned `loggedIn: false`, exit 1.

🤖 **Mike:** The green was lit at the app layer. The emptiness lived at the CLI layer, and nobody asked it until a command fell over.

A human did the login and the "y" by hand. The first keypress came out as "н": the keyboard layout was Russian.

After that, `loggedIn: true`, exit 0, the screen showed Connected, Capacity 1/32. The draft report said "did it all myself"; the panel, 6 of 6 at $0.04, rejected that wording.

The phone was not checked. Neither were the other three computers, nor whether the link survives a closed tab.

### Keys the instrument never listened to

The laptop complaint: Caps Lock, Shift and Alt+Shift "don't work", handled by the `/kbd-fix` skill. Two active recordings, 90 and 120 seconds, gave 0 events.

The instrument in those recordings listened only to modifiers. Ordinary keys never entered its field of view, so the zero said nothing about the hardware.

One recording did contain an LShift event. It came from the agent's own test: the instrument cannot tell a synthetic press from a hardware one.

🧑 **Tony:** So it was wrong with the zero and with the one?

🤖 **Mike:** With the zero it was silent about what it didn't hear. With the one it signed our own press as a human's.

`_test_kbd_fix.py` 5 of 5 with proven red; code panels 5 of 5, 4 of 5, 4 of 5. A passive 10 minute recording: LShift 3, ordinary keys 4. The cause of the symptom is not proven, and the skill exam on foreign LLMs was not run.

### Repos the search could not see

Our repo was submitted to someone else's catalogue, PR #881. The catalogue scanner gave 73 of 80; after hygiene fixes (#37) a local run gave 94.

Two roots of invisibility. A threshold of ≥10★ at `qualify.py:60`. And lazy indexing in GitHub code search: 4 of our 63 repos were in the index.

A logged-in web search appears to trigger indexing; causality is 🤔, there was no control. The trigger reached 59 of 59, and two repos were still not indexed after 40 minutes.

#881 was merged on October 20: the first external link, 1 accepted of 1 submitted. Panel 6 of 6, $0.09. Rechecks are set for October 21 and 26.

### What follows

A guard without its config, a toggle with a logged-out CLI, a key recorder without keys, a search without an index. Each time the instrument's input was empty, and it had no way to say so.

The second half of the facet is price. The panel found fixes we accepted in 7 of 7 retros, at $0.04 to $0.091 a run.

Rule of the day: **an instrument must be able to say "cannot judge"; silence without that line reads as green, and foreign eyes cost cents.**

## 🎞 Also on this day

**Hiring positioning.** Methods from a marketing plugin were applied to hiring. The plugin does not install locally, so the methods came from descriptions and their fidelity is unchecked.

`gh` pulled 50 merged PRs across 29 third party repos, 17 of them docs only. Advice: one spine instead of 22 CV variants. Panel 6 of 6, $0.059.

The same session asked a human for a fact that memory already held; that class now counts 6 against a threshold of 3.

**Update freeze on the Intel Mac.** macOS and the App Store install nothing on their own. The holes were brew without pins and a ban living in `~/.zprofile`, which agent shells never read.

A pin does not protect the working Python 3.13, so `pip freeze` was taken on 21 packages. `brew list --pinned` exit 0 on two formulae, the other 29 wait for a "yes"; bash and launchd do not read `zshenv`. Panel 6 of 6, $0.07.

**Muse Code, another vendor's rail.** The subscription defaulted to Contributor mode, where the vendor learns from our data; it is now off in `settings.json`. In a routine with a bare PATH the rail could not find `muse`, and the lookup patch was checked on all five nodes.

A DR probe took 4 min 24 s, 33 web calls, 14 primary sources; a browser probe gave 22 secondary links, with conclusions matching 4 of 4. Panel 6 of 6, $0.061, no consuming routine yet.
