🧑 **Tony:** And who wrote to the vault after that?

## 🏆 The main story

### The tripwire, the counter and the empty safety net

#### Two guards, both right

The first guard, the tripwire, sees many staged deletions and refuses. Its job is to keep a backup from silently locking in a mass loss.

The second guard, the maintenance gate, lets heavy work run once per day per node. Its job is to keep background work from eating the machine.

🧑 **Tony:** Where did they meet?

🤖 **Mike:** In one state file. The tripwire does not know its refusal spent the quota, and the counter counts attempts instead of successes.

#### What grew in three weeks

On September 29 the tripwire saw 171 staged deletions. On October 21 it saw 723.

Not found by basename: 139 then, 85 now. Days without a successful full backup went from 2 to 24.

Of the 723, 638 were found elsewhere: a relocation wave. The other 85 are renames with an id-scheme change, and all of it is recoverable from git.

#### Why nobody noticed

The task card that described the broken backup sat untracked for 22 days. It existed, and no board could see it.

🤖 **Mike:** The harm was written down, and the write-up was lost the same way everything is lost without a backup.

#### What was done

A scoped snapshot captured that card itself. A new P1 card was raised, and evidence was appended to two existing ones.

The full backup stays blocked. The `--force` flag is irreversible, the decision belongs to the owner, and the agent does not go there without it.

#### What is missing

The quota may be split across five counter files keyed by hostname: a hypothesis, untested. The sum of the two guards still has no owner.

### The same seam nearby

#### Folder policy and queues

The folder policy rejected good links 4, 1 and 6 times on October 19, 20 and 21. The deploy-unpack backlog grew from 6 to 8 packages.

Thirteen `.stversions` folders are still owned by root; 171 are fixed. Inbox debts fell from 227 to 6, and the total now reads 382 with 381 paid and 1 unpaid.

#### A doorbell that woke the wrong session

There were six background turns. The doorbell always woke one pinned session, whatever the topic was.

The fix is a dedicated address for the owning session. The doors file has the new door, and the canary passed read, reply and ack.

🧑 **Tony:** First try?

🤖 **Mike:** No. The model invented its own read command and dontAsk blocked it. The exact command in the template plus allowedTools on three commands fixed it.

#### What stayed red

A doorbell test reports SHA drift: expected 3AE16873, now E5B692E2. It has been red for about 36 hours, and there is no re-pin card.

The door health check says GRAY, "cannot judge 3 of 3". The permission fix passes the permmode test, and the fail-closed question has no recorded decision.

### The "lost" sessions turned out healthy

#### The weekly limit as a class

Three sessions were cut by "You've hit your weekly limit", resetting October 23. They were handled as one class.

One connector shows connected on its own page and is absent from the session status. Hypothesis: the app reads connectors at session start; untested.

A rail-nudge hook matcher lacks Write|Edit, and that became a card. A vendor-independent force-push gate architecture stands on exactly one node.

#### A slot that hid three jobs

A reused slot hid three jobs: telemetry of 13 KB, an arXiv paper and a draft of 1640 words, 11545 bytes. The exam session was closed by its own retro, with the answer key kept out of reach.

Protocol proposals ran 121, 13 and 6 in July, August and September. Over the same stretch the bus grew from 5781 to 10309.

There has been no arXiv submission for 15 days. Both sessions stopped at "browser without login" and tried no workaround.

🤖 **Mike:** The work is done, the consumer is standing at a login wall. One blocker, and it is the login.

#### A false batch

Three routine runs on October 19 shared only a name prefix and a date. They touched different files and were never a batch.

The fleet-script mailer test is green, rc=0. Delivery to peers is unverified.

#### Two batches on one slot

Two routine batches ran on one reused runner slot. The first was a five whys on session-conflict handling: the cause was proven for the board, skill conflicts stayed a hypothesis, and the card went up to P1.

The second was a first patch-oracle measurement of a security benchmark, pushed to a public audit repo. The gate "zero findings without instrument coverage" caught the run's wording.

### What follows

The tripwire is right, the gate is right, and the vault has gone three weeks without a full backup. One guard's refusal ate the other's quota, and neither of them sees it.

Rule of the day: **the sum of guards needs an owner; a quota counts successes, and one guard's refusal must be visible to the other.**
