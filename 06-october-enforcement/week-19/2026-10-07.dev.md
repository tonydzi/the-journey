# Day 126 - 2026-10-07: a verdict is only true about the surface that produced it, and the surface was chosen by reachability

Dev-log form. One pattern in eight instances, plus one human counter-example.

The unifying failure mode of the day: an instrument produced an affirmative verdict about a subject from the single surface it could reach, and the verdict was consumed as a statement about the subject. In every instance the verdict was literally true about that surface.

Position on the line. Day 124: a record of a thing was accepted as the thing, so existence claims went unprobed. Day 125: a record of required behaviour was accepted as enforcement, so control claims went unprobed.

Day 126: the scope of the probe was accepted as the scope of the world, so **negative** claims went unprobed.

Scale of the day: 35 distinct sessions, 166 calls, 113 of them robotic, 27 retrospectives, 12 vault commits, 45 config commits, 1 commit to this book, 2 decisions, 9 research orders.

The defect family involved, `vyvod-po-odnoy-poverhnosti`, held 168 dated cases as of this day, the first on 2026-07-27. Two of the day's eight instances were new dated cases in it.

---

## 0. The shape of the defect

A probe answers `absent` when it means `not found on the surfaces I walk`. The two differ only when the instrument knows its own coverage, and almost none of them do.

Formally: the instrument computes `f(S)` where `S` is its reachable surface set, and reports `f(U)` where `U` is the universe. The error is silent whenever `f(S)` is non-empty, because a non-empty answer reads as a working instrument.

**Why reachability drives surface choice.** Every one of today's instruments picked the surface that was cheapest to read: a file timestamp instead of a content timestamp, a registry row instead of a mailbox, a heartbeat instead of a scheduler journal, a requested model name instead of the responding model's name. None of these were wrong choices at authoring time; each was the only surface the author had wired.

**Required output contract.** A verdict carries three fields, not one: the verdict, the surface set that produced it, and the surfaces known-but-unreached. The third field is the one that was missing everywhere today, and the one that makes a negative verdict legitimate.

## 1. The prior-art probe did not walk the vault

**Problem.** `prior_art.py` answers "have we solved this before" and is consulted before building. Its surface assembly omitted the vault entirely: no retrospectives, no knowledge maps, no decision notes.

**Evidence.** 27 surfaces walked, 0 of them the vault. On this day a pre-build recall nearly missed an existing solution to the same topic.

**Why no check caught it.** The probe returned non-empty results from its other surfaces, which is indistinguishable from full coverage at the call site.

**Fix.** Vault retrospectives and maps added to traversal; the report now prints uncovered surfaces as an explicit line.

**Pattern.** An index whose coverage is not part of its output cannot produce a sound negative. Treat coverage as part of the answer rather than as a diagnostic.

## 2. A regex knew one lexical form of an identifier

**Problem.** An event-triage lane matched `lu.ma` only. The corpus of 8996 events contained 2871 links in the form `luma.com`, invisible to the lane.

**Second defect, same lane.** Date selection used the start date only, so a multi-day event in progress at query time never entered the sample.

**Result after fixing the form.** 8 eligible events where 4 had been visible.

**Status.** The point-versus-interval filter is unfixed: one dated case, mechanism threshold not reached. Named rather than left silent.

**Pattern.** When an identifier has more than one published surface form, a single-form matcher reports the universe as the subset that happens to use that form. Breadth of a matcher is a coverage claim and needs a control match on a known positive.

## 3. Wrapper freshness was read as content freshness

**Problem.** CRM sensors rolled out to the hub returned 5277 lead mentions, 3909 warm, 5121 candidates, with rc=0 and a fresh report mtime.

**Cause.** The source the sensor reads was stuck at 2026-06-08. The freshness check read the wrapper's timestamp, which neighbouring processes move, rather than the newest record inside the source.

**Fix.** Two of three sensors were given named consumers (coordination chat, heat map). The third has no consumer and is recorded as open.

**Pattern.** `mtime` of a derived artefact is a claim about the writer, not about the data. A pipeline's freshness watchdog must read the newest record inside the source, and a sensor without a named consumer cannot be observed to be stale at all.

## 5. "The node is silent" was a claim about the transport

**Problem.** A signed parcel went unanswered for 22 hours and the conclusion recorded was "the hub is silent". 21 signed parcels stood without completion receipts for one node; 4 folders unshared; distribution matrix red on 6 of 6 pairs.

**Actual cause.** The hub applies signed parcels normally. Its sync was not pulling the bus folder: 0.00 MB in 25 seconds over a live connection, read timeout every few minutes.

**Also.** Two external review engines returned 16 and 9 findings; 2 of the 9 were refuted by live measurement, the reviewer being confidently wrong about a sync configuration method. Outside eyes answered from documentation memory, which is their own reachable surface.

**Pattern.** "Silent peer" and "undelivered to peer" are distinct diagnoses with distinct cures. Liveness must be read across rails; a single rail's silence is not the node's silence.

## 6. Four indicators measured traces of work, not the fact of launch

**Problem.** A nightly pair of routines was declared dead by three consecutive shifts, and the conclusion was written into canon.

**Actual cause.** The runner launched and died on a subscription limit: 2026-10-05T22:15:49Z in 2.7 seconds, 2026-10-03T22:15:46Z in 3 seconds on the weekly limit. The same texts exist on September 23 and 17. A second routine died 2026-10-05T00:11:29Z, exit 1, in 8 seconds.

**Root.** All four indicators in use (heartbeat, journal entry, candidates folder, file mtime) measure traces of work. Death on a provider limit and a no-show produce identical emptiness. The only owner of the launch fact is the scheduler's run journal, which nobody queried.

**Fix.** The rule was written into the shift instruction in the same pass: query the run journal before using the word "dead".

**Structural note recorded alongside.** That pair's cron is every two days while the instruction's step closes one day, so half the calendar is uncovered by design even when all runs are green.

**Pattern.** Separate three questions and never substitute one for another: did it launch, did it finish, did it produce output. Only the launcher owns the first.

## 7. A panel printed the requested vendor, not the responding one

**Problem.** A six-engine second-opinion panel printed the name of the requested model regardless of which model answered, hiding fallback substitution. Run: 6/6, 215 s, $0.47.

**Why it matters.** The panel's value is voice independence. A silently substituted roster makes "four of six agree" mean something other than what it says.

**Fix.** The output prints an explicit fallback line naming the responder. Roster v2 pinned as 3 engines from China, 1 from Europe, 2 from the United States.

**Measurements after.** v2 6/6, 181 s, $0.31; review of the fixes 5/6, 490 s, $0.45; retro panel 6/6, 319 s, $0.07. Key limit raised to 15 requests per day (soft 15, hard 20).

**Pattern.** Any component that may substitute a dependency must name the dependency that actually served the request. Otherwise every aggregate computed over it is unfalsifiable.

## 8. Unit-of-measure substitution, in a report and in our own breakage count

**Problem A.** A morning report carried the number 17, consumed as a count of applications. It counted companies. Rows of the stated class in the database: 4.

**Alongside.** 158 applications marked submitted without proof (122 newer than 7 days, 36 older). Two technical tests had real durations of 20 to 40 minutes against a recorded "an evening". One test with a 7-day deadline had lain unmarked for 28 days.

A separate claim of "no access" was issued on one empty search; a live probe returned 345 finds.

**Problem B.** A defect family's count showed 24 cases. A red probe of the keys in both directions returned an honest 5.

**Root.** The same reachability bias applied to self-measurement. A family counted by the name it was filed under, rather than by the defect, both inflates and deflates, and the "mechanism after the third case" threshold arrives at the wrong time in both directions.

**Pattern.** A number crossing a process boundary carries its unit or it is not a number. And the count that gates your own repair policy is itself an instrument that needs a red proof.

## 9. The rule of the day

**A verdict must carry the surface set that produced it and the surfaces known-but-unreached. A negative verdict without the third field is not a verdict.**

Operational consequences, all paid for on this day:

1. `absent` and `not found on my surfaces` are different outputs. Only the second is derivable without a coverage proof.
2. Wrapper freshness is not content freshness. Read the newest record inside the source.
3. Launch fact, completion fact and output fact are three measurements with three owners.
4. A component that may substitute a dependency must name the dependency that served.
5. Self-measurement is subject to the same defect, so the counter that gates repair policy needs its own red proof.
