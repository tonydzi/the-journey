# Day 125 - 2026-10-06: a rule that lives only as text does not execute, and its non-execution is indistinguishable from silence

Dev-log form. One pattern in eight instances, plus one human counter-example.

The unifying failure mode of the day: a constraint existed as text and not as an obstacle on the execution path. Seven of the eight constraints were written correctly. None of them blocked anything, and in every case the resulting failure surfaced as an absence of work rather than as a refusal.

This is the next step on the line of Day 124. Day 124: a record of a thing was accepted as the thing, so claims about existence went unprobed. Day 125: a record of required or forbidden behaviour was accepted as enforcement, so claims about control went unprobed.

Scale of the day: 42 distinct sessions, 246 calls, 140 of them robotic, 61 retrospectives, 17 vault commits, 54 config commits, 1 commit to this book, 3 decisions, 7 research orders.

---

## 0. The external measurement that anchors the day

A system card published by another lab alongside a new product measures two constructions of the same prohibition. Written as a warning in model context, the prohibition is bypassed in 15.0 to 17.4 per cent of attempts. Implemented as a separate gatekeeper on the execution path, it is bypassed in 0 per cent.

Applied to our own stack: our most expensive constraint class (money, irreversible deletion, third-party secrets) is specified in prose. An audit found 15 hooks holding that class at zero, so part of the constraint has a gatekeeper and part of it runs as context prose with a now-measured price.

**Why this matters for anyone building agent systems.** A prose constraint's reliability is a property of the reader's attention budget, not of the constraint. It degrades exactly when context is long, which is exactly when the constraint matters.

## 1. The rule lived in an alias, not in the shared file

**Problem.** A command family with six entrances shares one skill file; the push robot invokes that shared file. The required behavioural role (argue, object, hold the goal anchor, name a bad premise) was documented at one entrance only.

**Evidence.** Occurrences of the role's keywords in the shared file: zero. Every entrance other than that one alias, and every robot push, executed the version without the role.

**Why no check caught it.** The checks verified that the text existed. It did exist, in the alias.

**Fix.** The role moved into the shared file; a guard asserts its presence there and is red on eight distinct ways of removing it. A seventh entrance was added at the owner's request.

**Boundary recorded alongside.** The new right permits rewriting the **form** of a task statement (phrasing into a checkable shape, ordering, splitting, naming an acceptance criterion) and not the **bar** (goal, mandatory conditions, scope, quality). Test: does the client receive the same or less.

**Pattern.** A rule in a file that one of N entrypoints loads is a rule for 1/N of traffic. Presence-of-text checks cannot distinguish that from full coverage.

## 2. A lane's door was drawn: case-sensitive comparison against a lower-case key map

**Problem.** A funnel lane for peer companies produced zero new rows across multiple nights. The order to build it had been executed and lost twice.

**Cause.** Company names were stored in human capitalisation; the board map keys were lower-case; comparison was exact character-by-character. The lookup never matched.

**Second cause.** The geography filter did not know the short form of a city name, suppressing one company's 14 rows to zero.

**Result after fix.** +89 rows across two waves.

**Pattern.** A failed lookup reported as "zero new" is semantically identical to "the world has nothing new". Lane instruments must distinguish a zero-result query from a zero-match key.

## 3. A shared probe budget with no per-lane quota

**Problem.** Two lanes draw probes against one applicant-tracking vendor from one shared daily budget. The first lane consumed the budget whole; the second received zero.

**Why it was invisible.** The second lane had no quota and no line of its own in the daily report, so a zero-work night and a zero-budget night produced identical output.

**Fix.** Per-lane quota and per-lane report line. Night-wave result: 33 peer-company rows against 13; probe budget raised from 40 to 60.

**Side finding.** Three sessions on two machines built this one order in parallel. A coordination board exists and was read by none of them before editing the shared zone. Third occurrence of that omission within the same day.

**Pattern.** A shared budget without quotas is an unwritten first-come scheduling rule. It is correct code and an undocumented policy.

## 4. A locked lease read as a busy neighbour

**Problem.** The shared skill shelf for two neighbouring agents was going stale. The standing verdict was "those vendors have no door, build a watchdog".

**Cause.** Both vendors had a door. The workspace write mechanism requires an explicit per-directory opt-in that was not being passed, and the refusal surfaced as `BUSY`, which was read as contention rather than as a closed lock.

**Result after fix.** Shelf counts 216 → 286 and 218 → 286; dangling junctions 48 → 0.

**Rule.** Before asserting "there is no door", query the named door's own counter for whether it has ever answered with success. A refusal and a contention must not share a word.

**Pattern.** This is the inverse of the day's main defect. Elsewhere a rule failed silently; here a door refused and the refusal was attributed to somebody else's work.

## 5. A guard existed and was never called from the guarded step

**Problem.** The CV pipeline produced stale output for three days. A key from which per-vacancy role summaries are assembled had dropped out of the master file between September 25 and 28.

**Finding.** A test asserting that key's presence existed in the repository and passed when run by hand. The skill step it is meant to guard never invoked it.

**Result after fix.** 19 CVs rebuilt; the guard's invocation wired into the step.

**Pattern.** An uninvoked guard is worse than an absent one, because its existence answers the question "do we have a check for this" in the affirmative.

## 6. A publishing slot with no consumer

**Problem.** A lane that produces one short post for an external reader after each session had published zero across its entire history.

**Cause.** The slot was built without a consumer. No component was responsible for taking the finished artefact to a platform.

**Work done.** A form gate for those posts was built: 83 checks green. Five assertions inside its own test suite were found to assert nothing and were replaced.

**Review history.** An external reviewer rejected the gate three times consecutively and accepted the fourth revision. The rejections were reproduced locally; the vocabulary-based judgements of meaning were removed, leaving the machine gate to judge form only (emptiness, weighted length, links in body, dashes, hashtags) while the author must name audience and benefit in two explicit lines.

**Owner instruction.** Selling is removed from this lane entirely: no offer, no call to action, no request to contact.

**Pattern.** A pipe without a mouth is a pipe that passes every internal test. Name the consumer before building the producer.

## 7. A prohibition in a docstring expired while the code kept executing it

**Problem.** One job board was excluded by a prohibition written in a module docstring with an expiry date of October 2. The code continued to apply the exclusion until October 6, consuming submission slots against a closed door.

**Adjacent defect, same family.** A `last_seen` field was written by the board crawler and consumed as "we attempted a submission". Forty rows for that board stood in state `new` with zero attempts ever made.

**Rule.** Successes are judged from the submission record; attempts are judged from the wave files. Neither is judged from a crawler stamp or from prose with no calendar.

**Pattern.** A constraint with a lifetime needs a machine-readable expiry in the same place the code reads. A comment cannot expire.

## 8. Self-healing by restart produced a new identity

**Problem.** The sync watchdog detected a disorder on the hub and restarted the daemon. The daemon came up against an empty home directory, generated a fresh identity certificate, and lost every peer relationship.

**Evidence sequence.** `peers=5,tcp=5` → kill and relaunch → watchdog logs `self-healed` → `peers=0,tcp=0` held for four days while the indicator read as healthy. One shared folder's backlog stood at 172580 files; after repair, 22.

**Cause.** An empty peer dictionary rendered as "0/0 connected; changes: none", which is indistinguishable from a healthy idle state. The check lived inside the object it was checking.

**Fix.** An external floor the object cannot lower: with no identity certificate and no backup present, the daemon refuses to start rather than starting clean.

**Pattern.** Any self-healing action that can recreate state must verify identity before declaring success. "Zero of zero" is not a health reading; it is an absence of a reading.

## 9. Supporting work of the day, compressed

**Approval window abolished.** The content approval window moved from 24 hours to 0 by owner order. The project's own regression grid was defending the forbidden delay: the value 24 was wired into a test as an invariant, so the test failed on the correct behaviour.

15/15 after the fix, 3 red mutants. A lint over "one number in two homes" (rule text and code) is required.

**Logout roots, both internal.** Two causes: a maintenance script cut working cookies, and an age-based sweep of a temp directory removed browser profiles. The same sweep on October 2 destroyed six things including the interpreter's standard library on the hub. The first forensic trap set for the culprit was dead and silent, producing the rule: a new watchdog is born with a red probe.

**Peer-request lane.** New bus lane: a request addressed to many, the claim taken by the first responder, the right passing to the next on expiry or explicit failure. Zero model tokens to write; append-only journal state.

**Liveness across six rails.** The hub treated both Macs as dead: heartbeats stale 69 and 61 days while both had written to the bus 1.9 hours earlier. The indicator measured whether a watchdog was armed on the node, not whether the node was alive. Liveness is now the minimum age across six signals with a mandatory coverage line.

**Bus economics.** 1866 blocks per day, 84.2 per cent receipt acknowledgements, about 3.1 per cent assignments. Writing costs zero model tokens; reading the whole log costs roughly 199K. Conclusion: raise a session on a request, not on a message, and read the bus only through deterministic lists.

**Transcription language was pinned.** The retranscription tool carried a default language instead of auto-detection, so English audio was being translated rather than transcribed. After the fix, 64 per cent more speech recovered; our rail returned 9109 utterances against the vendor's 3597, with 2 hallucinated CJK fragments against 527.

**No carrier between two accounts.** 100 approved event registrations on one platform against 2 events in the calendar, because registrations arrive under one account and the calendar connector watches another. 90 events backfilled, two-hourly carrier added.

**A green watchdog over an incomplete set.** Six mirrors of external articles had been published for a month and were invisible on five public surfaces; the watchdog had zero reds because its input set was incomplete. Root of the recurring author-identifier defect: matching on a surname field dropped authors lacking it, and an internal "this is him" predicate overwrote a real third-party identifier with a name guess.

**Platform ban root, eight years old.** The root of a professional-network ban was 1442 invitations issued in one minute in October 2018, not a name-transliteration mismatch as previously assumed. 32017 contacts were recovered from historical exports.

**Outbound sender is a claim.** One zone has no MX record and a second address has been blocked at the provider since October 1, so a conference application went out from an unintended account. The sender is proven by the sent-mail header, not by intent.

**Self-rounds counted for the first time.** The continuation counter saw only commands present in the session transcript; an assistant-initiated round leaves no command and had never been counted. New counter: 40 checks, 17 red breaks, 6 empty assertions found in its own tests.

**Double agreement as a defect class.** The "three pushes then distillation" rule was proven in live use: 13 distillates overnight, 12 findings in a paired review, and neither reviewer found its own error. Class recorded: a conclusion both parties agree on requires more measurement, not less.

**Public copy drift.** A publicly distributed skill file stood at 6650 bytes against 8757 live. Distribution is a delta update into the existing location, not a new repository per skill.

**Platform note.** On this operating system a process-replacement call spawns a separate process while the parent exits with code 0 and empty output, which was breaking two instruments silently. Replaced with a blocking subprocess call on that platform.

## 10. The human counter-example: a measurement reversed its own order

The owner ordered an audio course to stop himself swearing. Measurement of his own corpus returned 0.10 per cent in written text and 0.14 per thousand words in speech, against a published norm of 3 to 7 per thousand, roughly an order of magnitude below the norm.

He read the number and rewrote his own instruction: remove the swearing from the second of failure rather than all of it.

**Why this is the counter-example.** Eight machine constraints held their own text as enforcement all day. The human held his own instruction as a claim and let a measurement overwrite it.

**Process defect in the same session.** Generation started without reading the coordination board while a neighbour held an exclusive claim on the zone. The board is read as the first command, before any processes.

## 11. The pattern, stated for reuse

A rule that lives only as text is enforced by the reader's attention. A rule that stands on the execution path is enforced by construction.

- One rule, one door, and only a thing on the execution path counts as a door. Canon is read, not called. A description describes. An uninvoked guard is absent.
- A door's refusal must be lexically distinct from silence and from contention. `BUSY` covering both a lock and a neighbour costs weeks.
- Every lane gets a quota and a report line, or a shared budget silently becomes a first-come policy.
- A constraint with a lifetime needs a machine-readable expiry where the code reads it.
- A rule shared by N entrypoints lives in the file all N load; presence-of-text checks cannot see per-entrance coverage.
- A self-healing action that can recreate state verifies identity before reporting success.
