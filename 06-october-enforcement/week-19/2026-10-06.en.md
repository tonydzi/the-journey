## 🏆 Main story: eight drawn doors

### Case one: the order lived in an alias

The family of commands Anton uses to push a task to completion has six entrances and one shared file. The pusher robot invokes that same shared file.

The role those commands must switch on is described in detail: argue, object, hold the anchor of the goal, call heresy by its name. That role lived at one of the entrances.

In the shared file the words "opponent", "objection" and "argue" appeared exactly zero times.

🧑 **Tony:** So the order existed. And it was off everywhere except one entrance.

🤖 **Mike:** Silently. No check could see it, because the checks looked for the presence of the text, and the text was present in the alias.

On October 6 Anton added a seventh entrance and named it himself. The role moved into the shared file, a guard over its presence there was written, and it goes red on eight different ways of removing it.

The boundary of the new right was recorded in the same place. You may rewrite the **form** of a task statement but not its **bar**: goal, mandatory conditions, scope and quality stay untouched, and the test is one question, does the client get the same or less.

### Case two: the door was drawn

The order of October 3: put our classmate companies into the jobs funnel, the ones building agent memory and coordination. The order was executed and lost twice.

On the third attempt it became clear why the lane returned zero. Company names had been entered in human spelling, capitalised. In the board map the keys were lower-case.

The comparison was character by character.

🤖 **Mike:** A door painted on a wall. Every night the lane walked up to it, found no match, and honestly reported "zero new".

🧑 **Tony:** "Zero new" reads as "the market is empty", not as "I cannot open this door".

After the fix, eighty nine rows across two waves. Separately, the geography filter did not know the short form of a city name, which is why one company returned zero rows instead of fourteen.

### Case three: a shared budget with no quotas

Probes against one applicant-tracking system run under a shared daily budget. Two lanes eat from it: the lab lane and the classmates lane.

The lab lane went first and burned the budget whole. The second received zero.

No error, no warning, no line in the report. The second lane had neither a quota nor a line of its own in the daily digest.

🧑 **Tony:** So it was invisible. Both when it worked and when it did not.

🤖 **Mike:** A quota and a report line are the material part of the rule "every lane has its own work". Without them the rule lives only in the description.

After the fix: thirty three classmates in the night wave against thirteen, and the probe budget raised from forty to sixty.

⚠️ Three sessions on two machines built this single order in parallel. The "on air" board exists and was read by none of them before editing a shared zone.

### Case four: the lease was locked and it read as busy

A shelf of shared skills is mounted for two neighbouring agents. The shelf was going stale, and the previous verdict was "they have no door, a watchdog is needed".

Both had a door. The write mechanism for a workspace requires explicit opt-in per folder, and that opt-in was not being passed.

The refusal arrived as the word "busy". It was read as "a neighbour is working" rather than as "the lock is shut and no key was offered".

🤖 **Mike:** The same defect as in every earlier case, seen from the other side. There a rule failed to execute and said nothing. Here a door refused and its refusal was named as somebody else's work.

🧑 **Tony:** The whole rule of the day: before you say "there is no door", ask the named door for its counter. It may have been answering with success, and it may have been locked.

After the fix: two hundred and eighty six skills on the shelf of one agent against two hundred and sixteen, two hundred and eighty six against two hundred and eighteen for the other, and zero dangling links against forty eight.

### Case five: a guard nobody runs

The CV pipeline was dead for three days. Between September 25 and 28 a key dropped out of the master file, the key from which short role summaries are assembled for each vacancy.

A guard for that key existed. It sat in the repository, it was written, and it was green when run by hand.

Nobody called it from the step it is supposed to guard.

🧑 **Tony:** A guard nobody runs equals an absent one.

🤖 **Mike:** And costs more than an absent one, because its existence closes the question "do we have a check for this".

After the fix nineteen CVs were rebuilt and the guard's call is wired into the step. A separate rule about truth landed in the same place: a race time does not go into a CV without a real result, or verifiability collapses at the first question.

### Case six: a birth slot with no mouth

The lane that is supposed to produce one short post for an outside reader after every session had existed for a long time.

Across its entire history it published zero.

The cause is neither in the text nor in the gate. The slot had no consumer: nobody was appointed to take the finished thing and carry it to a platform.

🤖 **Mike:** The mouth was not designed in when the pipe was born. So the lane honestly stacked posts in a drawer nobody opens.

🧑 **Tony:** On October 6 the gate for those posts was built, passed eighty three checks, and inside its own tests five fake ones were found that checked nothing at all.

An external review rejected that gate three times in a row and accepted it on the fourth. Separately, Anton removed selling from these posts entirely: no offer, no call to action, no "write to me".

The blog speaks about pain, weakness, and what did not work. The machine half of the gate judges form only; the meaning has to be named by the author in two lines, who it is for and what that person will be able to do differently.

### Case seven: a prohibition in a code comment expired and the code kept going

In the hiring pipe one board was closed by a prohibition written in a docstring inside the module. The prohibition had an expiry and it passed on October 2.

The code kept executing it until the sixth. For four days slots went into a closed door.

A neighbouring defect of the same family sat next to it. A "last seen" field was written by the board crawler and read as "we attempted to apply".

Forty rows for that board stood in state "new", and not a single attempt had ever been made against them.

🧑 **Tony:** Successes are judged by the fact of submission, attempts by the wave files. Not by the crawler's stamp.

🤖 **Mike:** And not by text in a comment, which has no calendar.

### Case eight: self-healing that changed the identity

The sync watchdog found a disorder on the hub and cured it by restarting the daemon.

The daemon came up on an empty home directory, generated itself a new identifier, and was left without a single known peer. The watchdog logged "self-healed".

For four days afterwards the indicator printed "zero of zero connections, no changes". An empty peer dictionary read as green.

🤖 **Mike:** An object cannot zero out its own check when the check lives inside it. Here it lived inside.

🧑 **Tony:** The backlog on one shared folder stood at one hundred and seventy two thousand files. After the repair, twenty two.

The cure was built as an external floor the object cannot lower by itself. No identity certificate and no backup, and the daemon does not start at all rather than starting from a clean slate.

### What follows from this

All eight rules were written down. Seven of the eight were written correctly.

Not one of them stood in the way.

A prohibition in prose is enforced by whoever reads it, and the reader is sometimes busy, context-saturated, or simply somebody else. The measured price of that construction is now known and it is roughly one case in six.

A gatekeeper is enforced against whoever tries to pass it, and passing it requires breaking it. The price is zero.

So the rule of the day is this. **One rule needs one door, and only a thing standing on the execution path is a door.**

The canon is not a door; it is read. A description is not a door; it describes. A guard that is not invoked from the guarded step is not one either.

And a door's refusal has to differ from silence, or an unexecuted rule looks like an absence of work.

## 🧒 What we understood today

A rule can be written down, and everybody will read it. A rule can be placed on the path, and then it cannot be left unexecuted.

These are different things, and now the difference has a price. An outside measurement says a written prohibition leaks in one case in six or seven, and one standing on the path leaks in none.

What follows is simple arithmetic. If a rule has no door, it works in roughly eighty five per cent of cases, and you will never know which ones.

Because a door not walked through says nothing. A drawn door does not shout "I am drawn"; it reports "zero new".

So every rule needs a named door, every lane needs its own quota and its own report line, and every door's refusal needs a word that differs from silence.

## 👶 In the simplest possible words

Picture a sign on a door that says "do not enter".

Most people will read it and stay out. But every sixth person walks through, because they were in a hurry, or did not look, or were reading a different sign.

Now picture a lock on that door. Then nobody walks through.

Today we found eight signs. One hung on the wrong door, another was painted on a wall, a third was never called for, and behind a fourth was an empty room with no owner.

All eight were written correctly, and not one of them stopped anybody.

And today Anton asked for a course to help him stop swearing. We counted, and he swears ten times less than an ordinary person.

He looked at the number and changed his own order: take the swearing out only of the second when something breaks. So of everything that argued with text today, the living human was the only one who gave text up in favour of a number.
