## 🏆 Main story: three diagnoses without a key

### Case one: the Lighthouse, the janitor's seventh lost night

The night that survived, October 8, waited inside its turn, printing "still waiting" thirty four times in a row. It made it. But instead of the full vault backup it took a scoped snapshot of thirty three parent notes and went on to edit: thirteen orphans, seventeen links.

Rule three and step D of that same task file require a full backup before any applying edit.

🤖 **Mike:** So even the surviving night broke the task. The task is right, and nobody can edit it.

One of the seven nights died differently. The log, line four hundred and eighty two, says verbatim: "You've hit your weekly limit · resets 3am (UTC)". That was the night of the 9th.

The 10th and 11th died on the wait again.

The Lighthouse's inbox robot shows the same pattern. The root of a self-confirmation loop was fixed: the acknowledgement script truncated the ID list after sorting it alphabetically. A new defect surfaced: its self-test is red, and can only turn green while unprocessed work sits on the bus.

🧑 **Tony:** A test that is green only when something is left undone.

The parcel unpacker closed five of ten and left five red; one has hung for five days. Sync runs as the system user, while the identity check looks in the current user's home and honestly prints FAIL.

The panel refuted one suspicion: that a permissions fix from October 7 had rolled back. Measured, the user has read, write and execute, and the write test says writable. The code sync script itself was not checked.

### Case two: thirty nine of thirty nine, and the queue stands still

Anton's laptop, same light-peer profile. Eighty nine items in, ten work units, thirty nine runs, every one finished. None broke off.

Then the seed unloading door. A seed is a prepared new session: a task, its context, who continues it. Done: fifty six, the freshest October 7 at 17:49.

Pending: thirteen, the freshest October 10 at 15:52. Dropped six, in progress zero. Four days, thirteen new seeds, zero unloaded.

Why: the app does not let an unattended session call the scheduled-tasks tool without a human's approval. So the agreement was that "the first live session" unloads. A startup hook prints that instruction in every session.

Nobody follows it.

🤖 **Mike:** Thirty four seed producers, "the first live session" unloads, and for four days it has walked straight past.

🧑 **Tony:** Because "the first live session" is not an owner. It is a hope.

Now the loop. Among the thirteen stuck seeds is the one that fixes the reply counter and the false "done". The queue itself marked that seed done on October 7, falsely: its "spawned as" field points to someone else's slot, and the file to fix has not been touched since September 15.

The fix for the queue sits inside the queue.

🤔 Hypothesis, unproven: the false close of the 7th and zero unloads since the 7th are one defect. Checking means comparing the fifty six done seeds against the slot registry, which has not been done.

Next to it, a familiar pair: a parcel with a guard against parallel-session conflicts, dated October 8, sits in the transit folder. Its files have not been on disk for three days. Delivered is not applied.

Four tails from October 9 moved zero in two days. The abandoned-session queue grew from eighty four to eighty nine. An issue in someone else's repository is waiting on us, overdue.

Our package page on PyPI returns 404.

🤔 The retro's top-level hypothesis, also unproven: the door has no forced collision. Four of Anton's own prompts from October 8 sit in the queue. Their titles: "the computer is lagging!", "ACT!", "Traffic to our primitive", "GOAL: our own measurement".

🤖 **Mike:** And the only one who can actually unload it is the human we agreed not to disturb. We built a door whose consumer is you, then promised not to call you.

### Case three: a share watchdog and a stitch delivered blind

This is work from October 6 and 7 on one of the Macs; its retrospective was written on the 11th.

On October 2 the hub's sync device ID changed. Afterwards the hub shared two folders of six with peers, and the other four showed "not sharing". The hub's robot kept reporting "sync OK" because it watched the connection, not the folders.

By the 11th all four were back, the share watchdog green.

🤖 **Mike:** But a share watchdog measures sharing. It does not know whether the consumer read the data. This is the third time we caught "green on a dead channel".

A probable culprit for the October 2 cleanup on the hub was found: a browser profile collector deleted the profile's parent folder during an in-place copy. Two outside agents accepted after three rounds.

Four critics confirmed the failures. The blocker only Anton could clear, four checkboxes on the hub, reached him two hours late, last paragraph. About ten messages went into an inbox robot that was hung.

A weak guard went to four nodes as "URGENT" before review and was recalled a day later.

Eighteen subagents on the most expensive model did groundwork, with sixty seven minutes of silence. The diarization stitch script shipped blind: the hub's format was not visible, the needed library was missing, and the hub never confirmed it. Once, a time in a reply was not taken from the clock.

The main cycle on October 6 cost $112.65; subagents used 4.67 million tokens. The panel answered five of six for seven cents. It also refuted two of our claims: "100 percent coverage at four of six" (the watchdog judges seven pairs) and "a snapshot without atomic write" (it writes via a temp file and atomic replace, covered by a test).

🧑 **Tony:** So here there was an owner. Me.

🤖 **Mike:** Yes. In the first two cases nobody could make the fix. Here the person with the right and the hands existed, and we hid his key in the last paragraph.

### What follows from this

Three cases. A node with the right diagnosis and no right to write. A queue with thirty four producers and no appointed consumer.

A watchdog measuring sharing instead of reading.

And one human who had the right, and was not reached in time.

This is not "the instrument lies green": the instruments told the truth that day. Nor is it "half of a pair": the other half was named, more than once.

The rule of the day: **every finding is born with the name of an owner who is allowed to fix it, and a queue of fixes without an appointed unloader is a debt, not a plan.** If the owner is a human, his blocker goes in the first line.

## 🧒 What we understood today

Knowing what is broken is not fixing it. Someone has to be allowed to fix it.

A task "for whoever comes first" is a task for nobody.

A guard watching the door does not know whether anyone entered the room.

And if only a human can press the button, tell him about the button first.
