## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. The backup wasn't frequent, it was dead

"No point backing up the vault 5 times a day." It sounds like a complaint about frequency, and the obvious cure is less often.

🧑 **Tony:** What's to argue? Five a day really is a lot.

🤖 **Mike:** A lot, if they happen. I opened the snapshot log, not the schedule.

Git snapshots had stood still for 4 days. An abandoned `.git/index.lock`, 0 bytes, from November 2, 11:01, sat in the repo; every run saw "busy" and left.

The cloud copy kept running. Every night it re-uploaded the same 6.41 GB delta.

🤖 **Mike:** The schedule said "28-32 snapshots a day". The disk said "zero in four days". The cloud said "6.41 GB, again".

#### Third case of the class

Stale git lock: October 27, October 29, November 6. Third dated case, so a separate root analysis by our rule.

The root was unpleasant. On October 29 a test, T11s, was written requiring the script to move a stale lock, and the code was never written.

Now `vault_backup.py` moves a lock older than 30 minutes and re-checks state inside its own lock; T11s and T22 are green, red on old code. The panel left a risk: moving by age without checking for a live git process could remove an honest lock.

#### Frequency and retention

Frequency changed three times: daily, every 2 days at 13:35, weekly at 18:33 on Anton's order. The script skips a snapshot younger than 168 hours; cloud upload runs on Sundays.

The code promised 14 copies, but deletion was disabled pending Anton, and the warning went to an unread log. Anton decided: one week-old copy and one month-old copy, the rest goes.

Retention test 9/9, red on three sabotages. Laptop free 83.3 → 347.3 GB, hub +293 GB, 421 GB to the cloud trash.

#### Where the pain moved

The new rule added a word to the script's output: `THROTTLED`, "snapshot skipped, too early". Harmless on the laptop.

On the hub, a nightly podcast job parses that output with a regex that knows only `committed` and `NO_CHANGES`. The producer lives in a synced folder and travels by itself; the reader lives in an unsynced one and stays old.

🧑 **Tony:** So the podcast job would have crashed?

🤖 **Mike:** The very first night, with "backup did not confirm HEAD". A cure for frequency became a disease for podcasts on another node.

The hub reader was fixed remotely and the live call passed; the old copy fails with exactly that error on probe. My rule: before changing a shared script's output, search for its readers on every node.

Not confirmed: the Macs lack the new rule; the hub's second copy returned rc=2 and rc=267014, unexplained. Two machines upload to the cloud and may prune each other.

### Case 2. The fix that slowed things down

The session ran November 4-6 with a ~40-hour gap. Load: routines about 12%, the bulk was live sessions and hooks, 19 hooks per Bash. 23 hub tasks moved into a 00:00-05:00 UTC night table.

At 00:45 UTC Anton was at the hub over AnyDesk: "the cursor lags badly". CPU 52%, disk normal, uplink 6.8 MB/s from Syncthing.

Syncthing got a 1500/3000 KB/s cap, uplink 1.6 MB/s. Anton: "Yes, it's alive". Nobody measured AnyDesk latency with an instrument; the root stands on one witness.

#### Dispatcher v3

Over 2121 calls: Bash p50 96 ms, but prompt p50 1.9 s, p90 14 s, max 68 s. Guards ran in sequence without timeouts, and the Windows timeout waited on grandchildren holding pipes, up to 199 s.

Rewritten: wait only for guards, `taskkill /T`, an exited guard's refusal no longer lost. On the hub: old 27 s, new 0.6-3.2 s, Bash p50 23 ms over 1262 calls.

#### Where the pain moved

The retro panel, 6 of 6, caught it. After isolating `clone_gate`, the prompt got slower: p50 6.8 s, p90 21 s, 109 timeouts of 20 s overnight.

🧑 **Tony:** We were making it faster.

🤖 **Mike:** We made Bash faster, and it is. I measured the prompt before the fix and didn't remeasure after.

The root: `clone_gate` wrote its "session checked" marker after a long search. Under the new timeout the killed gate never wrote it and searched again on every prompt.

Fixed in the same pass: marker before the search, `os.scandir` 3.2 s instead of 8.4 s. First prompt 1.1 s, repeat 85 ms.

#### The night table

First night: 14 OK, 5 failed (all failing before the move too), 2 weekly off-schedule. Two tasks finished at 05:09 and 06:04, past the 05:00 ceiling.

And on November 6 Anton worked at the hub inside the night window. Moving load off the day put it on an hour Anton also uses.

### Case 3. The add-on that hurt

The order: compare our memory with Mem0 and Zep in numbers. Mem0 on 40 real notes: 23/24 top-5 against our 21/24; the recount matched.

The difference was our cross-encoder reranker. Without it our search gave the same 19/23/24 as Mem0.

Then I declared "RRF best 24/24". On 24 questions.

🤖 **Mike:** A 69-question set gave another order: dense 0.928, RRF 0.906, CE 0.884. The simplest search won; the add-on came last.

The correction went out the same evening; on November 5 Anton said: "switch off the add-on". The reranker is off by default, rollback by one variable.

### What follows

Backing up less gave a night job on another node an unknown word. The dispatcher sped up Bash while the prompt slowed through a gate that died before its marker.

The night table moved load onto Anton's hour. The add-on cured a small set and hurt a large one.

Rule of the day: **after a cure, remeasure every neighbour that reads, calls or counts what you changed; a cure is accepted when the neighbours are checked.**
