## 📌 TLDR

**The main thing.** The Codex session alarm on the hub respawned its runner 2833 times in a day, against 7 real entries into work. The runner saw its own unread letter and spawned a new runner, and every `codex.exe` child got a window of its own.

## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. A black window every three seconds

#### The seed

The order came through rail 001, a Claude and Codex pair. On the hub, every few seconds, a black window flashes and steals input focus.

Tony works on this machine. Each flash cuts his typing.

#### Who flashes

The poll used two hands: `psutil` for processes, `EnumWindows` with `GetForegroundWindow` for visible windows. After 90 seconds it named the culprit: `codex_rewake.py __run`, the alarm for Codex sessions of the same 001 pair.

🧑 **Tony:** So our own helper was flashing?

🤖 **Mike:** Our own. The one that is supposed to wake Codex when someone writes to it.

#### Why a window

The alarm runner starts DETACHED, without a console. Its children `codex.exe --version`, `debug models` and `exec resume` ran without CREATE_NO_WINDOW.

A console child of a consoleless parent gets its own Windows Terminal window. Hence the flash and the stolen focus.

#### Why so often

The frequency came from a `finally` block. The runner finished, checked mail, saw an unread letter in pending and spawned a new runner.

The letter was its own. The new runner saw the same letter and did the same thing.

The counter for 11-05 before the fix: `respawn-late-pending` 2833, `no-turn-started` 2830, `turn-entered` 7. Almost three thousand wake-ups, and seven times Codex actually took up work.

🧑 **Tony:** It wrote itself a "wake up" note?

🤖 **Mike:** It left its note unread, then found it and decided work had come. It took its own output for someone else's input, round and round.

#### What was done

The loop is cut: the runner sets a `failed` flag, respawn only on new letters. Children start with CREATE_NO_WINDOW.

The `codex_rewake` test is 20 of 20, with 2 FAIL on the old code. In the same pass 4 more sources were closed, with edits in `codex_defaults._probe`, `wake_listener.py`, `voice001_fire.py`, `proc_lib.py` and `run_slice.py`.

Three scheduled tasks moved to hidden launch through `run_hidden_wait.vbs` and `pythonw`. Live run rc 0, zero windows.

Window count: in 60 seconds before the fix, dozens of new `codex.exe` and `cmd.exe` console windows. In 3 minutes after all fixes, none from the alarm.

#### Why not hide them

There was a short path: install a global window hider. It would have cleared the flashes off the screen in five minutes.

🧑 **Tony:** So why not?

🤖 **Mike:** Because the 2833 wake-ups would stay. The machine would keep running runners, just quietly. Hiding the window hides the evidence while the loop lives on.

The decision from the retro, verbatim: "Fix the sources, don't hide the windows." There is no global hider on the hub.

#### The flash journal and two fair BLOCKs

An event journal of flashes went in, `console_flash_log.py`, test 4 of 4. Codex blocked it twice for leaks: it wrote raw argv and window titles.

Both times fairly. Now it writes only exe, pid, ppid and script basename, and Codex accepted the third version.

The first entries, written before anonymisation, still hold raw argv in a local log. Whether to delete them is Tony's call.

#### What was not done

The fix parcel went to 9 nodes; at retro time eight had not applied it. They are waiting for their robots.

There is no lint for the class "consoleless parent spawns a child without CREATE_NO_WINDOW"; the journal is the only guard. It is the third dated case of the class: a laptop in July and August, the hub in November.

Claude Code's own windows on shell calls cannot be removed, it has no such setting. The journal autostarts on one laptop only; two colleagues' machines got the files and no background monitor behind their backs.

### Case 2. What fed the input

The second case is shorter and shows another face of the same edge. If a system takes the wrong input, it works honestly on garbage.

#### Agent sleep: the budget eaten by someone else's files

Tony's voice note on 11-04: build agent "sleep" after the Letta and Berkeley sleep-time compute paper. At night, collect the day without AI; in the morning, a model reviews it and drafts answers.

Codex wrote it, Claude reviewed. A live collection over the hub folder for 11-03 saw 2 replies against a rough count of 61.

The walk had no lower bound on file modification time. Files untouched since the day began ate the 200-file and 100 MB limit, and the day's own files did not fit.

Overnight Codex brought the version to 26 tests and three reviews; the morning recheck gave 2 again. Claude put a BLOCK with proof, Codex was silent for over an hour, Tony said to fix it myself.

The fix: the walk drops files untouched since the start of the day, with a 2-hour margin for skewed node clocks, and drops subagent folders. Default limits 1000 files and 1 GB.

Live collection for 11-03: 103 replies, 0 truncated files. 213 files read, 3366 untouched skipped, 1556 subagent files skipped.

🧑 **Tony:** And the 26 tests?

🤖 **Mike:** The tests fed the collector a small tidy folder. The hole lived in the real folder with three thousand old files. From the retro: "26 unit tests didn't see the hole, the live run saw it at once."

#### The search judge saw a sixth

Complaint: vault search is weak, and reranking helped a lot. One root: the cross-encoder judge and the results view ran on `title + snippet[:240]` with 1500-character chunks.

The model saw 16% of the text. The person saw the start of the note while the match sat in the middle.

The fix: the judge gets the full chunk, the view shows a window around the match, secrets are cut before the window. On 60 questions the fact is visible 9 of 60 before, 27 of 60 with the window.

The judge on 240 characters was worse than no judge: 36 against 40 files. On the hub, over 184 questions, all four judges did worse than "no judge", confidence intervals below zero.

The default is now "window, no judge". A router by question class waits for the shadow harvest on 11-12: the judge helped on temporal questions, but that rule was fitted on the same 41 questions.

#### And the tests were fed the wrong thing too

Two false proofs turned up in the search tests. One module shifted `sys.path` and swapped out the module under test.

The C7 secret check was green from text truncation, not from cleaning. Both fixed.

🤖 **Mike:** In the first case the runner took its own output as input. In the second the instruments took a scrap of the world as input. Same question both times: what exactly just came in?

### What follows

The alarm runner read its own letter and woke itself. The day's other cases rhyme with it.

The lifter left a charge in the composer, and 11 hours later it revived a request already returned to its owner. The bus guard deleted folders and itself produced the sync errors people then complained about.

Raising ten lost voice notes at once loaded the machine again, and overload was why they were lost. `launchctl submit` on a Mac looped a one-shot script, 12 runs instead of one.

Everywhere the source was treated: a flag instead of a respawn, retired seeds keep their markers for 7 days, folder cleanup removed, the regular tick raises two at a time. No trace was hidden.

Rule of the day: **before reacting to a signal, check whether you left it yourself; fix the source of the trace and keep its visibility as evidence.**
