## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. A seed dead from birth

#### What the reaper did

The reaper looks for routine runs the app lost. For each such task it queues a seed: "run the one-shot task again."

17 runs were lost over two days. There were plenty of seeds to raise.

#### Why the seed could not work

A one-shot task is disabled after firing. `run_scheduled_task` refuses a disabled task.

The reaper wrote the same seed for any task. By the time a session read it the task was already disabled, so the seed was dead from birth.

🧑 **Tony:** So the order was right, just not for the task in front of the session?

🤖 **Mike:** It was right for the task as it was before firing. The reaper looked at it one minute, the session at another, and in between the task changed state.

#### Done and not done

The code is fixed, test 36 of 36, 3 mutants. On 10-27 there were 3 dead seeds.

The fix is applied on 1 node of 8. A live seed on a new death hasn't been observed yet.

A second reaper lane also opened: the cron lane for limits. A cron routine died at a limit, and it needs picking up too.

### Case 2. A brief about four expired profiles

#### What was said

The brief arrived with the line "4 browser profiles of the audio service expired." The whole plan was built on it.

The line was inaccurate. The default profile is alive.

#### What was actually there

6 of 7 profiles are dead. Our own guard removed them on 10-12.

The venv has no playwright. On top of that, the lift queue raised a twin on the same job, and 40 minutes went to duplicated work.

🧑 **Tony:** Wasn't the brief written from a fresh measurement?

🤖 **Mike:** It was written from a measurement someone made earlier. On its way to the executor the number changed, and nobody knew the cause at all.

#### The outcome

Within one hour 14 login windows were opened, all DEAD. Exactly one bucket is alive.

Logging into the rest waits for Anton. Those are physical hands, and there's no going further without them.

### Case 3. A seed older than the order

#### The symptom

Diarization on long podcasts crashed with OOM. Memory ran out on the whole file.

A windowed mode had existed since 10-24. The public door still fed the model the whole file.

#### Where the condition went stale

The work seed was written before Anton's order of 10-28. The session carried out an order that already had a newer owner.

🤖 **Mike:** The session did what was in the seed. The seed couldn't hold an order that didn't exist yet when it was written.

#### What was done

The door is fixed, mutations 25 of 25. At night it runs with `--no-diar`.

The fix has no consumer yet. The door stands, and nobody has walked through it.

### Case 4. A pause newer than the schedule

#### The order

The tab-sweep routine: "works on all machines." The schedule check returned exit 0.

Exit 0 proved only one thing: the task is in state PAUSED. It exists, and it is standing still.

#### Why

Anton's pause of 11-01 is newer than the order of 10-23. The session read the old order and checked it as if there were no pause.

🧑 **Tony:** So is the routine broken?

🤖 **Mike:** The routine is intact. One version on 6 nodes, 0 FAIL. What broke is the conclusion "works," because Anton's newest word was "stop."

#### Outcome

Nothing closes on its own. Lifting the pause was asked in one line.

### Case 5. "No search exists," stale by evening

#### The claim

Two things were said about the speaker materials: "no search exists" and "the file never existed." Both went stale within one evening.

The search was built: 103364 vectors. Of seven queries, six found their target.

#### A glued quote

A quote without a timestamp glued together two phrases from different hours. At 14:29 it was "did not record," at 20:27 it was "recorded the video."

Read together, they make a contradiction that never happened. Six hours lie between them, and in those hours the world changed.

🤖 **Mike:** A quote has to be taken with its clock. Without it, two truths from different hours turn into one lie.

#### Where we are

A clip is published. There is no live camera recording yet.

### What follows

The reaper looked at a live task, and the session got a disabled one. The brief carried an old number, the seed carried a world before the order.

The schedule check didn't know about the pause. The claim "there is none" lived longer than it was true.

Rule of the day: **an order's condition is rechecked at the moment of execution; the order carries the date of its world, and the executor compares it with whatever is newer.**
