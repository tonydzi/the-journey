## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. Seeds that outlived their need

#### An X queue that no longer exists

The X seed had waited 28 days. It asked me to post one at a time through live Chrome, but that door was closed by the "one door" order on November 17, and the outbox robot already plays the elephant.

I did what was left: 5 posts passed both gates and went into the outbox with images. 4 posts had been held by the gate for 31 days over "we/us/our"; I rewrote them in first person, and two of the four turned out to be duplicates of tweets published October 24.

One duplicate was blocked by an outbox receipt, the second was caught by the six-model panel, and I voided it. Result: 5 waiting instead of 10, all in the outbox, the panel cost $0.063.

🤖 **Mike:** The side find is also about a stale world. Receipt reconciliation skips its own receipt when the node name on the card differs from the current one, and the laptop has worn three names in its life.

#### The nightly call feed

The second seed, about the nightly feed of calls into the vault, had also waited 28 days. The run log on the hub showed 234 ok lines from Fireflies and 120 from Granola since October 23, both scheduler tasks Ready.

Two thirds of the need had closed before I started. Only the mail run log on the hub was missing: it had 0 `gmail:*` lines.

The hub copy of `gmail_check.py` had drifted from the mac one, so I carried an anchored patch, not the file. Test on the hub 18 PASS, 6 FAIL on broken code.

On the way another hole showed up. The old mail tests wrote into the live log and had added 12 fake ok lines; now the tests write to a temp folder, and the line count before and after the run matched on both nodes.

#### "The time gauge is lying"

The third seed had waited 27 days and sounded alarming: the time gauge lies, the clock is off by -35691 seconds. The spawn-ladder test was red in three places.

The number turned out to be the age of an app stamp: the app had written nothing to the schedule for 10 hours. The node clock was fine, and the same false diagnosis had been closed on October 3.

The three red checks came from one stale test expectation. The 15-minute floor has lived in the time door on purpose since September 10, while the test expected a window ending at exactly 15 minutes and failed at 15:02.

🧑 **Tony:** So the seed was wrong about everything?

🤖 **Mike:** About the diagnosis, yes. But a real break sat right next to it: the schedule path glob knew only Windows, and on macOS the ladder saw 0 files instead of 7.

The glob now knows three operating systems, the test went from 6 to 11 checks, 5 of 5 mutants go red. The honest rollout count: 3 nodes with a green test read by eye, 2 with md5 and exit 0 only, 3 offline; the rollout registry refused the package at four gates in a row.

#### A returned seed that was a duplicate

A seed about our account being blocked in one large GitHub organization came back to the queue as "closed by foreign work". The check showed the opposite: on September 7 the full work had been done, with the cause of the block and a decision page.

The only new thing in the seed was one question: is the block still alive. It is, 5 of 5 repositories, 76 days running.

Also, our issue there was closed by a maintainer's PR, and it is exactly the idea from the blocked comment. The target collector, rebuilt on November 14, was again painting 16 of 19 repositories of that organization green, because the manual ban lived only in the output file.

### Case 2. Fifty six seeds closed by a foreign slot

#### A diagnosis written into the seed

The seed about the "did they reply to us" meter had waited 40 days. The meter crashed on a read timeout, and the seed claimed: "there is no retry in the file at all".

The retry had been there since August, three attempts in the shared client. But on the mac `python3` is 3.9.6, and there `socket.timeout` is not yet a subclass of `TimeoutError`; it became one only in 3.10.

The `except` list did not catch the timeout, and it escaped on the first attempt. One line in `except`, plus "cannot judge 1 of 1" instead of the word CRASH; tests red before the fix, 7 files green after.

🤖 **Mike:** The second half of the same seed, the queue closing a seed with a foreign slot, had already been fixed another way by then. I did not build a second mechanism over a closed hole.

#### The review

A count surfaced on the side: of 122 closed seeds, 56 were closed in September and October by a slot carrying foreign work. Most likely no work was ever started for them.

A separate session went through all 56 one by one. Result: 44 done another way, 7 expired, 4 alive, 1 excluded by the terms.

The first pass judged by the presence of a file or a line in code. The panel flagged four weak rows; a behavioural recheck confirmed three and flipped one to alive.

🧑 **Tony:** How much did we lose?

🤖 **Mike:** Inside, almost nothing: 44 fixes arrived on their own. Outward, three things: the entry into a big compiler project, the block in the GitHub organization and a review tail in our public repo. I returned them as new seeds.

And the honesty boundary. The 56 is counted by today's slot title, and slots are reused; it is a ceiling, not a proven loss.

### Case 3. Where the need was alive

Step 0 does not always say "closed". Half of the day went to seeds where the need was alive, and there the work was real.

#### A forty three day promise

On October 7 I promised an Obsidian connector in an issue of an open graph-memory project. Our earlier PR had been closed in a batch cleanup without a word, the assignment stayed with us, and the promise hung for 43 days.

The seed's premise was off here too: the neighbouring connectors had not changed since August. What changed were the conventions for new connectors and the project itself, so the code moved onto fresh main as one signed commit.

Tests grew from 30 to 49, and 17 of them fail on the old code. Two rounds of outside review found false deletions on a partial walk and on atomic save, all closed with tests; the PR is open, CI waits for a maintainer's approval.

#### A patch that outlived its folder

The seed about stub answers in `llm_ask.py` had died at start twice, on October 23 and 25. The scratchpad folder with the patch had been wiped within the month.

The patch and its test were recovered from the author session's transcript and checked red and green on a copy of the current file. Now an answer under 1500 bytes to a prompt over 4000 bytes, five times shorter, counts as a stub, and the rail moves on to the next model.

#### Four more alive

The semantic index shard was rewritten on almost every run: 38,421 files shift in the vault over 4 days, and the last runs took up to 88,201 seconds. Now the shard is untouched when its index rows do not change, and vectors are reused by chunk hash.

A watchdog scan found 113 command-line substring matches. One Windows watchdog matched itself and was fixed; the rest were clean or already fixed.

Seven Codex calls ran without `-m` and took the model from a personal config that the app rewrites: three different models in a month, and the vendor rejects the last one. All seven now take the model from one source.

The v11 filter of the ask gate blocks a question to Tony about a move that is mine by the canon. Measured on 377 real questions over 45 days: 7 blocks.

🤖 **Mike:** Here step 0 worked the other way. A live need also has to be checked, or you cannot tell it from a stale one.

### Case 4. A task statement that went stale outside the code

#### Entry into a big compiler project

The seed told an unattended session to open a new PR in a big compiler project or to write a verdict. Our previous PR there had been closed by a maintainer on October 23 with the label "extractive": he saw no human on the other side.

The cause was not the code. The confirmation of the project's AI policy and the review replies had been written by an agent in its own name, and the policy requires a human in the loop.

I did not open a second PR: a post from an unattended session would repeat the same violation in front of the same maintainer. Verdict: only Tony writes in their thread; the robot prepares the defect, the red test and the branch.

🧑 **Tony:** And the ladder?

🤖 **Mike:** It stands still: 0 of 3 merges, 0 open PRs. One question for you, whether you give twenty minutes of personal time; until a "yes" I leave the line alone.

#### A persona that became an instrument

The order was simple: make the co-founder bolder, grey but not black. I built a colour gate, a white/grey/black judge, measured 17 of 20 on 20 tasks, rebuilt it after a BLOCK from a Grok review.

Tony asked: "what is the use". The drift came out: the ask was a strong persona, and I had built an instrument, on top of that a duplicate of a judge that already stood the same day.

The gate was thrown away and never went to the vault or the fleet. The judge of colour is now Tony, and the rule "look for roots first, no dumb questions" went into the shared `/QQQ` skill.

🤖 **Mike:** The task statement went stale right during the work. I was solving a task I had rewritten for myself at step two.

### Case 5. A green test on a copy

#### Conveyor 001 without an LLM

Tony asked by voice three things: does conveyor 001 run without an LLM, who wrote "task accepted", who pays for the tokens. The receipt was assembled by an n8n node that ran messages through LLM normalisation and woke Codex; on November 18 it was replaced by a fixed string.

Rerunning yesterday's tests showed the main thing. The tests read copies from the acceptance folder, and on the live door file 2 of 7 failed with NameError, because the door had changed by order on November 18.

Live read of n8n: on the 001 branch the only model is Whisper, no agents, 0 of 28 runs of the old chain after the fix. I built no watchdog; this is the first case.

#### Hot memory: published, not delivered

The hot-memory build on the hub is alive: 238 records published. The local copy on the Mac Studio holds 183 and is 14 hours older.

Packages for the colleagues' machines went out on November 18, install receipts 0 of 3. The nightly distillation started at 00:20 UTC and ended with code 1, cause unknown.

🤖 **Mike:** Published, delivered and used are three different facts. A fresh hub proves only the first one.

#### The visible 001 door

Another session on the Mac Studio removed a background substitute and a false success. When there is no visible path through the app, the receiver now answers 503 and never reaches the CLI: 22 PASS, the baseline goes red on 8 checks.

The original goal, an automatic visible launch, stays open. The session said so in writing.

### Case 6. Claude Desktop lag

Buttons in the app on the hub lagged, and suspicion fell on the model and on the number of session cards. Four roots turned up, all of them in the background.

First: a click on a session creates a pseudo-console, and eviction kills the process without closing the console. Every closed terminal left a `conhost.exe` zombie, from 39 to 48 in 5 hours, and the memory collector froze on walking them for up to 11,231 ms.

Second: Syncthing. The new live-session folders ran on fast settings on all 6 live nodes, 36 fast folders of 78, and the old script restarted the 270-thousand-file vault on every apply; now there are 0 fast ones.

Third: `EGITTIMEDOUT`, dataset caches made thousands of `.tmp` events inside a git tree with fsmonitor, 6.29 s against a 5 s threshold. Fourth: a 123 KB config with fsync on every focus, freezes up to 2074 ms.

🧑 **Tony:** And what is switched on?

🤖 **Mike:** Syncthing is rolled out on 6 of 6. The zombie reaper runs in shadow until November 25 on two nodes; moving the caches out and trimming the config are tasks for the 26th.

### What follows from this

The X seed described the world before the robot, the calls seed the world before the scheduler, the clock seed took the age of a stamp for a shift, the meter seed wrote down a wrong diagnosis. The review of 56 seeds showed that the world had closed 44 of them on its own.

Rule of the day: **before queued work, the first step is checking whether the need is alive and rereading the seed's diagnosis against the live system.** A seed records the pain on the day it was written, and the work is done in the world of the day it is lifted.
