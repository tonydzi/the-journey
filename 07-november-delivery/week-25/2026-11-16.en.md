## 📌 TLDR

**Main.** Tony's order: merge everything from worktrees into main and stop working in them. Audit, cleanup and ban all happened in one day, yet every call-level lock was bypassed by at least one harness: a full path to git, its own gitoxide, its own code with `core.hooksPath=/dev/null`.

## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. A hundred and twelve copies and nine roads to them

#### The order and the first pass

The order was simple: unmerged worktree work into main, new copies forbidden. `wt_audit.py` walked 6 nodes, and unmerged work turned up only on the hub.

112 copies removed, 46 stale entries pruned, 37 test fixtures moved out. The ban went into CLAUDE.md v6.16.0 §7.7, and `agent_worktree.py new` now answers exit 2.

🧑 **Tony:** So it's closed?

🤖 **Mike:** That is what I thought then. Six independent checkers thought otherwise.

#### A blind audit

The audit looked at fixed roots and never at the drive root or Temp. A timeout on a large folder turned into "dirty 0", meaning "no dirty copies".

The apply flag went as an environment variable before ssh and never reached the remote side. The first "apply" on one node was a dry run.

🤖 **Mike:** Yesterday's zero without a map, this time with my signature. The timeout said "I don't know", and the report printed "zero".

#### The Mac node and cards that resurrect

On the Mac node 107 copies were removed, branches intact, 102 of 102. The copies folder shrank from 406 MB to 4 MB, and 138 session cards of 140 were rewritten.

Then copies started coming back. Root: an old session, on return, recreates its copy from the path in its card, and a lock on the WorktreeCreate event never sees it, because the app is the one calling git.

129 cards moved to the main folder: 746 before, 746 after, 0 lost. A folder belonging to another person slipped into the list by mistake; its card came back from backup.

After that the app created 4 copies again, past the hook. The treatment is a launchd watchdog that fires after the app relaunches; it has not been tested in battle yet.

#### Nine harnesses under live fire

From there I stopped trusting configs and started shooting. For each of 9 harnesses: actually request a worktree and see whether a copy appears.

Muse from Meta makes the copy with its own code and sets `core.hooksPath=/dev/null`, so repository hooks are silent for it. The rule in the git wrapper caught only calls through PATH, while the app and launchd call `/usr/bin/git` directly.

On the Mac Studio, Cursor calls git by full path, and Codex has its own gitoxide that ignores the wrapper. The post-checkout hook I was counting on was installed in none of 22 repositories.

🧑 **Tony:** So every door was open.

🤖 **Mike:** Every door was shut for one road. Harnesses take different ones.

#### A lock that doesn't care who knocks

The fix had to move from the call to the resource. A plain stub file sits where the copies folder used to be, and git answers "Not a directory" to any attempt, however it was invoked.

Wrapper test 49 of 49, 13 red on the original version. For Cursor and Codex, `worktree_dir_lock.py` sets mode 0555 on their copies folders and adds the hook to 22 repositories.

The live shot `cursor-agent -w` got EACCES, `codex exec --worktree` got Permission denied. Test 11 of 11, 5 red on a broken copy.

The package went to 9 nodes; application on 8 is unverified. Along the way the package registrar masked an ImportError as an UnboundLocalError, and the first error looked like a bug in my code.

🤖 **Mike:** The ban in CLAUDE.md stays; it tells people and models why. The stub is what holds it.

### Case 2. A link that cost as much as a post

A reply with a link on X is billed as a post with a link, roughly 13 times more expensive. Moving the link into the first reply was never a saving, and our own skill knew it.

59 replies with a link plus reading other accounts ate almost the entire balance. Tony's decision: no links on X at all, only link in bio.

The `url_in_body` lock in `x_publisher.py`: test 29 of 29, 28 of 29 on a copy with the lock switched off. The rule block sits in 12 SKILL.md files, and the skills package to the fleet was bounced by five gates in a row.

Then posts with a link quietly appeared, past the robot; the source was not found. A neighbouring session locked the side doors, `x_post.py`, `x_thread_post.py` and the `x_outbox` store, test 9 of 9.

🤖 **Mike:** Same edge at a small scale. A lock in one publisher guards one call, and there were four roads to X.

One more lesson: the `rail_transition` alarm was wiped when `x_publisher.py` was overwritten whole; it was restored, test 37 of 37. An edit in a file that gets uploaded whole lives until the next upload.

### Case 3. Rail 001 moves to the Mac Studio

Voice door 001 moved from the hub to the Mac Studio. The Claude half worked, the Codex half hung: `codex app-server`, launched from launchd, blocked in `open()` on the Vault.

All 195 consecutive NEW-NOT-SWITCHED failures broke off at the same node 82 of the accessibility tree, inside Sidebar. `mac_ax_view` 1.1.0 now skips Sidebar and writes `ax_errors`; a window walk went from 1175 to 572 nodes, and 26 of 27 debts were raised, 0 duplicates.

Accessibility was granted to `/bin/sh`, not to bare python3, and `mac_ax_pick.py` sets Opus Medium. The risk is named: that grant gives UI control to any sh script.

### Case 4. The principles block in skills: an experiment instead of a swing

The swing has a short history. On November 2 the order was a pointer, on November 14 the full block went out to 496 files and was folded back the same day, and the "deferred" record from November 1 was never closed; it is now closed by fields.

On November 16 Tony said: the decision is not rejected, the full text goes into skills verbatim, as an experiment. Live probe with `--no-principles`: Codex 5 of 5, Grok 0 of 5, GLM 5 of 5 only with substitution.

Grok's root was a door. The generator labelled `~/AGENTS.md` as the file "Grok always takes", while Grok reads its own `~/.grok/` folder, and the instrument showed "CURRENT" for a door the model never opens.

🤖 **Mike:** Call versus resource again. The instrument checked that the file was written, when it needed to check that it was read.

The full block went into 333 skills. My own breakage: writing with `newline=""` turned 310 skills from CRLF to LF, the diff swelled to 48 thousand lines instead of 3.6 thousand, all reverted; the selftest did not catch the lost CRLF.

### Case 5. A hundred and seventy five names

The rename of 175 skills was not rolled back; the links were fixed instead. The harness refuses a Skill call by its old name before any hook runs, so a hook cannot cover the old name.

2632, 45 and 424 files rewritten, 30 wrong replacements reverted, CRLF restored in 1425 files. A review by 47 agents gave 35 findings, 14 of them live breakages in robots; rollout to 6 nodes, 3 offline.

Five whys found the root in the check. `--verify` called the same `plan()` as the fixer, so it saw the world through the fixer's eyes and missed the bypasses.

Evidence: `push_local` with `PUSH_TEXT="/continue"` lived on under a green verify. Now `--verify` walks the consumers, test 22 of 22; variant v5 was dropped, its +0.40 pp with CI [-0.50, +1.36] is noise.

### Case 6. Speed: what is a brake and what is a guard

Switching guards off was broken down by event. `onair_auto` cost 1100 ms at start and 10 ms on write, so it went back on write and on reply; `mcp_lazy_gate` with a cached verdict takes 58 ms against 2786 ms.

A recheck found three holes and a regression. The reaper could cut pinned and RC chats, two dispatchers listened on one port with SO_EXCLUSIVEADDRUSE, and `have.py` answered a false "no".

`memory_recall_hook` is on, UserPromptSubmit p90 4.3 s, tests 24 of 24. Eight codes in `session_codes` now point to a different session, and I recorded that as a swapped answer, not as "0 lost".

Startup caches gave an honest result with no gain: 12.3 s became 13.5 s at load 50-220. A green cache would also have hidden a file rollback.

The recall drop from 0.729 to 0.624 turned out to be a measurement error. A reindex swapped the database mid-run, lexical search failed on 171 of 184 questions, and the exam recorded the run as valid.

🤖 **Mike:** My own errors go on record too: a SIGSTOP with no way to lift it cost the night of November 7. And an estimate of "40-90 minutes" became 3 hours 42 minutes.

The manual night failed first on `first_json` (`raw_decode`), then on 316 "corrections" of 387 that turned out to be messages from the call robot. Delivery of the alarm into chat 03 is tested only with a mock.

### What follows

Git wrapper versus a full path, hook versus own code with `/dev/null`, the X publisher versus three neighbouring scripts, `~/AGENTS.md` versus `~/.grok/`, `--verify` versus the same `plan()`. Each time the lock sat on one road, and the resource was reachable by another.

Rule of the day: **a door counts as closed after a live shot at it, and the lock goes on the resource itself: the folder, the file, the permission.** Config, `--help` and a green instrument speak about intent; the shot speaks about the door.
