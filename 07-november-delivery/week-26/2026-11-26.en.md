## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. A switch in the next room

#### What was slow

Hooks (small programs that run at session start) on the ZBook worked like a plug in a pipe. `inbox_hook` took 8.9 seconds and timed out 27 times.

`memory_guard` returned an error on 464 starts of 465. `deploy_check` timed out in 26% of starts.

The start-up hint weighed 18.7 KB. The model saw 2 KB of it.

#### Where the previous fix went

Earlier another session had already switched these hooks off. It set `disabled:true` in `hooks/hook_dispatch.json`.

But the ZBook wrapper reads a different file, `state/hook_dispatch/config.json`. The check back then went through a third door and honestly showed green.

🤖 **Mike:** The journal already holds 70 cases in the family "fix without a live check". Today's goes into the same family.

#### The fix on a "yes"

Tony said "yes", and I switched off 23 hooks: 20 at start and 3 on every message. Four cheap guards stayed, each under 25 ms.

The check went through the same wrapper the system calls. Result: 449 ms, 6 hooks, 893 bytes of hint.

A red probe on the old file: 15 640 ms, 26 hooks, 14 634 bytes. The panel of six models answered 6 of 6 and asked for one thing: log which config the dispatcher read.

#### My slip on the way

I put the first edit in through a heredoc (a block of text typed straight into the shell). It gave a SyntaxError, and the wrapper was broken for about a minute.

I rolled back from the backup and did it again. The backup before editing a shared file paid for itself within a minute.

🧑 **Tony:** That's it?

🤖 **Mike:** No. Incoming messages and packages are now invisible at start. And I measured 449 ms on a synthetic run; the live starts are still ahead.

### Case 2. Edit and check in one pass

#### Who ate the time

A neighbouring session on the same ZBook counted what hooks ate on November 25-26. On steps 1249 seconds, on starts 1426.

The main eater was `memory_author_stamp`. It runs after every shell command: 1078 calls at 376 ms each.

Inside, every command was checked against 2310 regexes (text search patterns). A simple substring check went in first, and the median became 61 ms instead of 387, the slow ones 81 instead of 683.

#### Numbers from live starts

Start over 19 live runs: 10 234 ms became 472 ms. First message: 2468 ms became 358.

Test 13 of 13, the red probe gives exit 1. The "after" sample is small, 42 calls, and this is not rolled out to other nodes.

The panel removed my prefilter. It lost files with an upper-case `.MD`.

#### A false green three times

This is where I tripped. I sent the edit and the check in one parallel pass.

The check read the old file before the edit had landed. Three times in a row it showed green on something that did not exist yet.

🤖 **Mike:** The family "instrument lies green" holds 62 cases. Two small ones of mine join it too: I wrote a time from my head, and I named a count of found sessions before the search had run.

The rule is written as a recipe: **first the edit, then in the next pass the check, through the door the system calls.**

Both surfaced because we checked through the door people will actually use. The same door exposed one more thing: the rail plan lock had dropped out of the hook dispatcher on the hub and the Macs.

🤖 **Mike:** That is the family "built it and never connected it", 70 cases. And something next to it is burning: images for X posts have not been drawn for 96 hours, 16 posts are waiting.

### Case 4. A backup behind a fuse

#### Thirty four days

The copy of disk E to disk F had not run since October 23, 34 days. A fuse blocked mass deletion.

By the twenty second, 184 745 files were queued for deletion against a limit of 5000. The robot knew from November 1 and held a P0 task, and for 25 days nobody acted.

#### A copy without deletions

I made a copy without deletions: 2 hours 15 minutes, 2 443 870 files, 834 GB, exit 11. 617 places did not copy: 465 access denied, 150 vanished on the way, 2 busy.

Spot check 100 of 100 by SHA256 (a file fingerprint). The run on the 26th with quarantine stopped itself: it counted 184 764 paths against 184 763.

🤖 **Mike:** The root was found and fixed on the 27th. And for three of the seven "failed" hub routines, the first cause I named from the last log line turned out wrong.

#### Failures that never failed

Of 69 routines the instrument called failed, 41 do not write a log at all. Scheduler codes 267009 and 267011 mean something else and do not count as failures.

Of the seven examined, one was fixed in code. That was `corpus-bench-weekly`, which had held a broken copy of its repository since October 2.

### Case 5. A rule past the cut byte

#### A file that gets cut

We measured how often answers hold the three supports of §5.4: what the conclusion stands on, how it was checked, what would refute it. Antigravity on ordinary turns gave 12.2%.

The root is simple and annoying. `GEMINI.md` weighs 127 972 bytes and is cut at byte 23 959, while §5.4 sits at byte 75 887.

The model simply never saw that rule. The file was there, and the piece that mattered had no reader.

#### What changed

The supports and "Before -> After" went into the first 20 lines of the shared rules block. The guard now covers 13 surfaces, 44 of 44 red mutations, rolled out to 6 nodes.

A repeat measurement over 56 Antigravity sessions: 58.5% became 83.0%. On /QQQ, 1 of 4 became 14 of 15.

🤖 **Mike:** The panel caught my denominator error. The first count mixed 47 final answers with 101 pauses and gave 36.5%.

### Case 6. A night script that always said "ok"

#### Zero of 720

The instrument showed 0 answers of 720. The night run had not started for eight nights, and the harvest on the 25th came in at zero.

On failure the script always returned exit 0 and kept quiet. From outside it looked like a calm night.

#### A loud failure

A scheduler task now runs at 00:10 until December 15. On failure the script returns exit 10 and shouts into chat 03.

The probe gave 8 of 720 plus 8 more in the second wave. A real night is not verified yet; the first run is on the 27th.

### Case 7. A model chosen but never read back

#### A courier that died on someone else's limit

The podcast courier died on "Fable limit reached". The seed asked for Sonnet, and the session ran 144 turns on Fable.

The lift door picked a model and did not read the label before Send. On the twenty fourth, two of four lifts with a Sonnet seed landed on Opus.

#### A check before the button

Now before Send the door reads the model from the screen and compares it with the one ordered. No match means MODEL-UNSAFE, and the button is not pressed.

Tests 12 of 12, 3 red on the old door. A 7.5-hour stall of the request holder remains: the cleanup robot did not tick, the laptop was asleep.

### What they share

The hooks were switched off in a file the wrapper does not read. The rule sat past the byte the model never reaches.

The night script said "ok" with the same code as on success. The door picked a model and never looked at what it got.

The rule of the day: **the check goes through the door the system really calls, in a separate pass after the edit.**
