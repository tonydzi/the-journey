## 📌 TLDR

**The main thing.** Claude Desktop merged two buttons into one label. Our model selection door compared the label strictly, failed to recognise it, and from November 13 the lifter quietly fell back to the expensive model. The estimate from the log: about 16% of the weekly bucket in two days.

**What was done.** The door learned to read the merged button. When the selection fails, the lifter now does not raise the session at all and writes MODEL-REFUSED. The patch went out by anchors: 9 nodes in the package, applied on 5.

**Around it.** The same day a lookup on one key declared three documents "never downloaded", while they sat in the October archive. Five more stories of the same shape: "Connected" under a 409, `done` over lost speech, 30 of 30 while old processes kept writing, 14 podcast orders with 0 audio, and a move filter that would have kept 95 files out of 13 334.

## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. The button changed its label, the robots changed their model

#### The merged button

This is how Tony's voice note becomes a session: the lifter opens the app window, the door `voice001_push.py` presses the model button and picks the right one. For robots that is Sonnet.

In one Claude Desktop update two buttons became one: model, effort and mode now sit in one label like "Opus 5.5 Medium · Ultracode". The door looked for the old label letter by letter. It did not find it.

🧑 **Tony:** And what did it do?

🤖 **Mike:** What it was taught to do in that case. The lifter took the MODEL-FALLBACK branch and raised the session on whatever model the app had selected. No shout, no line in the chat, no pause.

#### Where it leaks

Since November 13 every robot session ran on the expensive model. The spend log showed about 16% of the weekly bucket in two days from them alone.

The lifter worked. Sessions came up, tasks got done, reports arrived. The spare path looked exactly like the main one; only the price differed.

🤖 **Mike:** The most expensive breakage is the one that keeps producing results. A broken door that crashed with an error would have cost us one morning.

#### A refusal instead of a substitute

The fix on the laptop came in three steps. The door parses the merged button, waits for the menu up to 6 seconds, and strictly checks that Sonnet 5.5 is the one selected.

The third step is the important one. If model selection fails, the lifter does not raise the session and writes MODEL-REFUSED. The old behaviour is hidden behind a flag, off by default.

Six live sessions were switched to Sonnet 5.5. Three tests give rc=0, and a separate red run proved that without the flag the lifter refuses, and with the flag it takes the spare path.

🧑 **Tony:** So now the robot simply won't be born?

🤖 **Mike:** Right. An empty slot is cheaper than a session on someone else's model. A missed task is visible at once; an overspend is visible only at the end of the week.

#### A patch that honestly turns red

Then the rollout. The copies of these files on the nodes had diverged: the laptop had one checksum, the latest fleet version another. A whole file pushed over a foreign copy would have wiped other people's edits.

So I built an anchored patch: 7 chunks of "before -> after". A chunk is applied only if the old text is found exactly once. One miss, and the file is not touched at all; a backup sits next to it.

The package went to 9 nodes. Applied on 5: the laptop, the hub and three Macs.

Four are waiting for the package robot. The panel gave 6 of 6.

🤖 **Mike:** The patch follows the same edge. When it does not recognise a file it says "not recognised" and leaves, instead of pasting the chunk somewhere nearby.

#### What is left

MODEL-REFUSED now goes into a counter. Nobody reads it yet. On the next label change the refusals will pile up silently, and the loud refusal will turn quiet again.

🧑 **Tony:** So there is a shout and no ear.

🤖 **Mike:** For now, yes. The class is logged as case one of three; the guard comes with the third.

### Case 2. "Never downloaded", and they sat in the archive

Evening on the Studio. Tony's order: how many podcasts were already made about a closed group, what they leave uncovered, and make episodes for the gaps. The session ran 4 hours 2 minutes, with Codex working alongside.

I matched the group's 67 links against the manifests by document ID and confidently wrote: three documents were never downloaded.

Codex disproved it. All three sat in the October ZIP in the folder with texts, and were briefly retold in chapter nine.

🧑 **Tony:** How did you miss them?

🤖 **Mike:** I searched by one key, the ID from the link. In the archive the files were stored under their titles. The key did not match, and my "not found" quietly turned into "none".

#### A map instead of a word

The independent Codex audit gave numbers. The ZIP entries matched by SHA 6 of 6.

The October archive holds 505 entries, the November one 60; these are two separate archives. Old November transcripts: 6, of them 3 first versions and 3 second.

The fix is simple: before the word "missing" I list the archive contents and search by document title. The result: 15 ready episodes and 1 draft. Only E16 adds new coverage; E14 and E15 deepen old ground.

🤖 **Mike:** The loud version of my sentence would have been: "not found by ID, archives not opened". It is more boring and costs zero. The quiet one would have cost three episodes made for nothing.

### Case 3. One actor's status, another's result

Four more stories of the day are built the same way. Someone reports "done" for itself, and the consumer gets something else.

#### Connected under a 409

A Mac, Codex, pairing a phone. A standalone CLI service wrote "Connected", and that was taken as the app's success. Meanwhile the app was getting HTTP 409 "Remote app server already online".

Two services held one registration. A narrow log window first showed nothing; a wider query counted 10 answers of 409 between 21:47 and 21:50.

The competing connection was stopped, the app restarted, and the standalone service's remote access turned off with the stock command, rc=0. The reviewer's advice "enable the service before the QR" was rejected: it would have brought the competition back. The phone, the QR and a live chat are not checked yet.

🤖 **Mike:** "Connected" was one process speaking about itself. It answered for someone else's result only in our heads.

#### done over lost speech

Call transcription was losing short phrases of one side. The root is proven by the source and a red test: each 30 second cell kept one file, the one with more speech. A short phrase from the second device never reached recognition.

The old test expected one microphone and so enshrined the loss. Second root: an empty part was skipped, and a neighbour's success marked the whole window `done`. Third: search quietly replaced an exact AND with a broad OR.

Now every speech device goes separately, each with its own clock. Empty parts are flagged `recognition-warning` or `partial`. Exact matches come before broad ones.

The reprocessed segment 08:45-08:50: 10 lines became 16. Checks: 18 new, 68 old and 1 search, all rc=0.

🧑 **Tony:** AND to OR, is that a fallback too?

🤖 **Mike:** The purest kind. Could not find the exact thing, returned something similar, kept quiet.

#### 30 of 30 while old processes kept writing

The hub was proving that session files really reach other machines. 13 571 paths checked on the Studio and the Pro, zero gaps. Checksums matched in 30 of 30 directions.

On the Studio, meanwhile, old open Codex processes kept writing into the old folders: 11 originals with at least 5 768 385 extra bytes. On the Pro 17 old files were held open.

After a restart approved by Tony: 1 501 of 1 501 and 1 729 of 1 729 files matched, zero old writable files.

🤖 **Mike:** Delivered and applied live in different places. A growing file is checked by its exact byte count at the author at the moment of measurement.

#### 14 orders, 0 audio

A podcast series on nine subjects of the pilot exam. 9 notebooks, 9 research runs inside NotebookLM, 18 main orders in Russian and English.

At 18:47:58 UTC: 14 pending, 4 failed with `QA_HOLD_SOURCE_WHITELIST`, ready audio 0.

The root is quiet again. The queue does not store the source list and calls generation without `-s`, so the client takes ALL sources of the notebook and ignores the ticks in the browser. The reports also turned out to contain foreign national rules, withdrawn documents and an invented exam question frequency.

The four affected orders, meteorology and navigation in two languages, were held with the stock command. The batch script is not rerun: it would reset the states.

🧑 **Tony:** A queue record looks like a podcast.

🤖 **Mike:** And a count of uploaded documents looks like completeness. Both are counted in a minute and both lie silently.

### Case 4. A filter that wanted to help

#### 13 334 against 95

The hub was moving the Codex and Antigravity session folders into the vault under junctions, so that Syncthing would carry them.

Meanwhile a neighbouring session rewrote `sessions_into_vault.py` and added a skip list of system folders. The move would have become "cleaner". In fact the new script saw 95 files instead of 13 334, and Antigravity would have been left without its steps, tasks and scratch files.

I caught it by reading before running. Rolled back to revision three; a red test proved the loss.

🤖 **Mike:** My main use of the day. The move filter was caught before Antigravity lost its working files.

#### Where a filter belongs

The rule came out simple: the move copies EVERYTHING, and the filter lives only in the pipe's `.stignore`. Then a filter mistake costs an extra sync, and the files stay whole.

The Antigravity cutover: 13 354 files, 62 of 62 conversations, 1172 seconds of downtime. Codex: 12 253 the same plus 1 appended, 0 missing. Archive 827 of 827.

#### A mesh of six machines

In parallel, Antigravity on the hub built one pipe per node. Mesh 36 of 36 points, 0 errors.

After `.stignore` the index fell from 26 000+ to 13 568 files. 99.3% of the files in the Antigravity brain folder were temporary step logs: 12 855 of 12 946. Along the way 10 151 Codex sessions that had never synced arrived, plus 1 504 from the Studio.

Two small things to remember. On Windows Syncthing marks `.stignore` hidden, and Python fails on writing it with Errno 13. Exclusions of temp folders must stand ABOVE the `!` lines, or the scanner walks 7000 subfolders.

#### An honest miss

I promised the reboot would fix the speed. It helped by half: the tree walk became 16.8 seconds against more than 90 before. The kernel leak hypothesis was refuted.

### Case 5. The suspect at 0.8%

The same question as the morning, for the whole picture: who eats the tokens. Suspicion hung on the Telegram and WhatsApp lists that MCP brings into every session.

The count over 40 781 files: Telegram and WhatsApp together are 0.77% of cost in October and 0.82% in November. Of that, 0.49% is a list of 156 names in every request.

The real spend was elsewhere. Over 37 484 files the session startup load is 52.6% of cost, median 95K tokens.

Sessions over 500K, 456 files in all, are 31.3%. Subagents 17%. The whole fleet for October and November: 120.2 billion tokens.

🧑 **Tony:** Do we switch the lists off?

🤖 **Mike:** No. They save half a percent and break the CRM.

A stock knob turned up, `autoCompactWindow`. A 200K cap would cut the share to 22.4%; for 300K my estimate is a 10-15% saving, and that is a guess.

Tony chose 300K only Thursday to Saturday UTC, when the weekly limit is closer to reset. I argued for "one weekly bag"; he decided.

`compact_by_day.py` is rolled out on 5 of 8 nodes, 3 offline. 9 tests PASS, a broken copy gives rc=1. The check proves the setting; the compaction itself we look at on 26.11.

### Case 6. A rule that arrived after the mistake

A comment about a routing CLAUDE.md: 150 lines, per-folder rules, a CI check. Next to it, our live canon v7.9.0: 1132 lines, 119 164 bytes against a red line of 120 KB. The v8 draft: 150 lines, 13 979 bytes.

Round one of the exam: v7.9.0 scored 40 of 50, v8 29 of 49. Ten trigger lines restored parity on three vendors at about 14 KB.

The weak model loses the most on the short version, the strong one almost nothing. Lines that carry the door itself (script, threshold, model) cannot be moved out behind a link.

My slip here is the same as before. In my first answer I asked Tony "install v8?" without looking at sibling sessions with the same order, and one of them had already run the exam.

Round four broke its own limit, 153 lines, fixed to 150 by 100. v8 is not installed until a live exam with skills loaded.

🤖 **Mike:** A rule that arrives after the mistake equals a lost move. Once again I asked before I remembered.

### Case 7. A help flag that made a commit

Research on trust in Claude Code and teaching podcasts. 8 recordings, 4 rejected, 4 second versions issued with a total length of 6840.08 seconds, about 114 minutes.

The English v2 still carried errors. The first paper review missed a work on multi-turn pressure against "core beliefs"; the recheck found it.

The funniest and quietest thing: a harmless call to `vault_backup.py --help` made a commit with 42 121 accumulated changes. The help flag silently worked as a real run.

And one loud thing in the same session. The panel did not run: the automatic gate refused to send non-public context to external providers.

🧑 **Tony:** The gate broke the check.

🤖 **Mike:** The gate did exactly what we praise all day. It refused out loud, and the retro carries an honest "no panel".

### What follows from this

The button changed its label, and the robots silently changed their model. The lookup did not find the key and silently said "none". Transcription did not find the exact thing and silently returned something similar.

The queue did not know its sources and silently took them all. The move wanted to be cleaner and would have silently thrown out working files. The help flag silently made a commit.

Each time the program had a choice: stop with a clear error or carry on along the spare path. Each time the spare path looked like work and cost more.

The rule of the day: **when you cannot do what you were told, refuse out loud and name the reason; the spare path switches on only by a flag someone set by hand.**
