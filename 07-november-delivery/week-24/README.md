# Week 24 (Nov 9-15) - the week a thing is asked where it came from and who signed that it arrived

> *A fix is only yours when its bytes are yours. A receipt is only true when the consumer wrote it. This week asks both questions of things that were already marked done.*

The week opens with Day 159 (November 9): an automatic rollback on a laptop that took a backup made on the hub, with 85 hub paths in it and none of the local ones, a fix that lived on one node while the hub walked without it, and a night "dream" that wrote lessons nobody had verified under a judge's borrowed name.

Day 160 (November 10) asks the mirror question: a queue, a forecast and a watchdog that stamped "done" at the moment of intent, so the receipt arrived before the fact.

Day 161 (November 11): a verdict that held on a single line. "Three times more honest than Mem0" was one refusal threshold (3 of 22 without it, 10 of 22 with it, at the price of 12 extra false refusals out of 69), and a stop-hook, an exit-code check and a browser watchdog each hung on one hidden setting too.

Day 162 (November 12): the remedy with a knife. A reindex with `--force` wiped a live code index to zero, and the first two versions of the worktree hook could delete a copy holding someone else's work; a test and a second panel caught both, and a door in the code closed them.

*Week 24 is closed: all seven days (November 9-15) are written.*

Day 159 (November 9): a fix, a rollback or a signature arrived from another machine or writer, and the house accepted it as its own until it learned to ask where the bytes came from. Day 160 (November 10): queues, forecasts and watchdogs stamped "done" at the moment of intent, so receipts now come only from the consumer.

Day 161 (November 11): honesty of a claim, a hook and a watchdog turned out to be one threshold, one word list, one `set` line. Day 162 (November 12): our own repairs nearly erased other people's work, and each time a test or a second panel stopped the blade.

Day 163 (November 13): every loud claim of the day rested on something we had added ourselves (a refusal threshold, a reranker, a CUDA flag, a re-enabled task), and each shrank to the truth once that addition was switched off. Day 164 (November 14): "nothing found" without a named scope reads as "nothing in the world", and the same zero without a map turned up in five harness slots, a Kaspersky search, a recall of "all sessions" and a podcast that voiced 749 thousand unmatched links as "broken".

Day 165 (November 15): a repair changed one place while the test, the mutants, the installer parser and the family counter stayed on the old anchor: a hook entry that silently did nothing under Git Bash, 15 of 22 guard tests red for ten days, and a signal audit that read 9 lines from 568 tasks.
