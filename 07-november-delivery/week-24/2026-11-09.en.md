## 🏆 Main story

### Case 1. A rollback made of foreign bytes

#### What broke

On the ZBook, routines kept coming back empty: 12 runs of 9 routines plus 1 manual run, 13 blocked sessions. The auto-rollback `hook_dispatch_build.py revert` picked the hub's backup.

That backup held 85 paths with "Users\Anton" and 0 local ones. The rollback restored the file faithfully, and the file belonged to another machine.

🧑 **Tony:** It was rolling back to a working state.

🤖 **Mike:** The hub's working state. On the laptop those paths open onto an empty room.

#### Lesson

A rollback checks where a backup came from before it checks how fresh it is. The freshest backup from another machine loses to yesterday's own.

### Case 2. The dream signed with someone else's name

#### What the dream does

At night the agent "dreams": it rereads the day and writes lessons into memory. Those lessons went in unchecked.

The judge signed with another rail's name. The file fingerprint was taken after the check, and one lesson was written twice.

🧑 **Tony:** Then who signed the lesson?

🤖 **Mike:** One rail's name sat on another rail's answer. Memory trusted the name.

#### What we put in

The judge now signs with the rail that actually answered. The fingerprint is taken at the moment of the check, the lock is set before the "3 edits per night" limit, and a lesson is written once.

Dream tests OK. Red is proven for items 1, 3 and 4; item 2, the fingerprint, has no red proof.

### Case 3. A fix that lived on one machine

#### The gap

The `llm_ask.py` fix from 10-19 lived on the MacBook. The hub ran without it: on 10-23 the hub lost its scripts, and nobody compared.

🧑 **Tony:** But we fixed that.

🤖 **Mike:** On one machine. The rest of the fleet counted the fix as its own by hearsay.

#### The instrument

`fleet_file_parity.py --alarm` now runs inside Fleet Parity Watch. md5 `dd922071` sits on 5 nodes, and the instrument prints "cannot judge 3 of 9" by itself.

### Case 4. A merge that erased a fix

#### The fork

Two versions of `proc_patrol.py` lived on the ZBook, 930 and 1204 lines. They were merged into md5 `87e777b3` (r2), tests 88/88, 38/38 or 36/36, 63/63.

The live run killed 2 of 7 sessions. Both had been silent for 25.7 h and 20.5 h.

#### Neighbours on the thread

The 10-22 merge erased the 10-06 fix. The other side of the fork came in whole and carried the newer line away.

r2 went to 5 nodes with a red neighbouring test. 7 of 9 nodes did not apply r3, limit-reaper runs the right md5 on 3 nodes of 7, and Studio could not see its own queue.

🤖 **Mike:** A merge brings foreign lines in with your own. Only a test that turns red can tell them apart.

### Case 5. A foreign python under launchd

#### What we found

On the Mac Pro, launchd starts jobs in a bare environment. There the system `/usr/bin/python3` answers, and our code never asked for it.

`cred-entry-watch` logged 1427 `bus_failed` since 11-04. 27 plists checked, 3 places fixed.

🧑 **Tony:** It failed quietly for almost a week?

🤖 **Mike:** Loudly, every time, into a log. Nobody read the log.

### Case 6. A night watchdog rewrote the logon

#### What broke

The Codex Desktop watchdog killed the app and never brought it back. 17 restarts over 10-27 to 11-08, 16 of them fail.

The lifter task and the watchdog both sat in LogonType Password. After the switch to Interactive, tests 62 passed.

#### The root

The nightly `os_task_watchdog.ps1` at 11-07 04:30:18 moved the lifter task to Password. Then came 180 skips in a row with one reason: "screen locked."

🧑 **Tony:** The watchdog was fixing a task we wrote.

🤖 **Mike:** By its own template. Its signature landed on top of our setting.

In the live run after the fix: 52 lifts and 113 false "twins" across 15 seeds. The twins still wait for a look.

### What follows

Six times a foreign thing came in through a door we built for our own: rollback, memory, rollout, merge, interpreter, scheduler.

Rule of the day: **before you accept bytes, a signature or a rollback, ask where they came from: whose path, which rail answered, what md5 the neighbour runs.**
