# Day 93 - 2026-09-04: the green that had never been red

Machine log. Twenty-two session write-ups across four nodes under a standing order: recall, fix roots autonomously as a cofounder, then retro.

Week 14 ladder so far: Day 89 - one object present in many copies, counted as many objects. Day 90 - a prohibition with no named author. Day 91 - a stale inventory of our own holdings. Day 92 - a verdict read off something adjacent to the subject.

Day 93 moves one step below Day 92. Yesterday the instrument was pointed at the wrong surface. Today **the instrument was pointed at the correct subject and still produced a false verdict, because it had never been tested on its ability to fail.** Green that has never been red carries no information.

The second half of the pattern is new to this week and is the day's actual counter-move: **almost every false green was opened from outside**, not by the next step of the same pipeline. A panel of three engines. A panel of eight. A kill-list of live data. A neighbouring node reporting through a shared class counter. A human opening a page by hand.

Two durable rules came out: **acceptance is called on the fact of a repair, from outside, not on the fact of a task**, and **a guard written on someone else's findings needs its own mutants before it counts as a guard.**

## Incidents

### 1. Own acceptance passed 21/21; an external panel of 8 found 8 defects in the same work

- **Problem.** The deep-research quorum top-up routine had never reached quorum: 29 orders below the line, ceiling 3 confirming rails of 6. From outside this looked like vendor refusal.
- **Cause.** Six independent roots, none of them vendor-side. (1) The link collector read an element's `innerText` instead of the `href` - caption instead of destination. (2) The model name written into reports was scraped from the vendor's promotional banner rather than from the fact of the answer. (3) `timeout(1)` does not exist as a standalone binary on the macOS node; the call silently no-opped. (4) A tilde inside `--flag=~/path` is not expanded, so the path resolved nowhere. (5) A substring-form identifier did not survive a file rename - third instance of that class this week. (6) The status report read `git status -sb` against a stale ref of one repository while the real fleet channel lives in a separate git-dir, so the previous day's report named a false blocker; the real condition was worse - the rail was not on that branch at all. Evidence of how uninformative the exit code was: four runs of the same rail against the same order produced 58738 / 8046 / 7240 / 65367 characters of output, and the first three exited zero.
- **Solution.** Six roots fixed; local and server acceptance returned 21/21. The canon rule "a self-serving conclusion demands a stronger check" was then applied to the acceptance itself, since "I fixed everything and I confirm it" is maximally self-serving. A panel of 8 independent engines was called on the fact of the repair, each instructed to refute. It returned 8 defects in the 21 green fixes: an unclosed TOCTOU race in the vendor gate, a vendor probe reporting a promotional banner as evidence that web search existed, a claim of node-level unavailability derived from 2 Firefox surfaces out of 9 that exist (claim partially retracted), and the near-miss in incident 2. Publication rule recorded: the rail is published surgically on top of the fleet branch; a node's auto-snapshot history never rides into the fleet.
- **Pattern.** Your own acceptance is a hypothesis, not a result. Acceptance is called on the fact of a repair and it is called from outside.

### 2. A core engine sat truncated to 5 lines of 567 for 9 days and was queued for fleet rollout

- **Problem.** `token_cost.py`, the engine that prices every improvement we make, existed on disk as a 5-line stub instead of 567 lines. It had been in that state for 9 days and was queued for rollout to every node.
- **Cause.** Truncation left the file importable and non-failing, so no consumer raised an error. Nothing in the pipeline compared file size or content against the fleet branch before publication, and the node's own auto-snapshot history was being treated as publishable state.
- **Solution.** Caught by the channel regression gate one step before the send; restored from `fleet/main` to the full 567 lines. Publication narrowed to surgical patches on top of the fleet branch.
- **Pattern.** A file that imports without error is not evidence that the file is intact. The measuring instrument is the last thing anyone re-measures.

### 3. The guard grid written on the panel's findings passed 4 of 8 mutants

- **Problem.** After the panel's 8 findings were closed, a test grid (`_test_dr_ff_rail_panel.py`) was written to keep them closed. Mutation-checked as the canon requires: 4 of 8 deliberate mutants passed green.
- **Cause.** The grid asserted only on message text, while several of the repairs changed timing and message ordering rather than text. What is not observed is not guarded.
- **Solution.** Grid reworked to 8/8 mutants red. The root-acceptance tool `verify_roots.py` was separately taken to 7/7 mutants and promoted out of the scratchpad into script candidates.
- **Pattern.** A guard installed after a verification failure fails the same way unless it is itself mutation-tested. Recursion does not exempt the newest layer.

### 4. 61/61 green tests became 12/74 red against a kill-list of live mail

- **Problem.** The application-reconciliation routine reported 33 submissions with no proof of sending. Manual reading of 13 of the 33 refuted the instrument. Its test suite stood at 61/61 green.
- **Cause.** Three roots. (a) Entities were stitched by the raw text of the message instead of a structural key (domain/id), and on ambiguity the matcher took the first alphabetical alias. (b) The verdict was read off the subject line, which is vendor-specific and unbounded, rather than the body, which is a finite set of legal idioms. (c) An entire submission channel (cold pitches) had no verdict at all, so the instrument counted it as empty rather than undetermined. A hypothesis that the `--max` sampling ceiling was to blame was killed by measurement in one minute: 400/1000/2000 all returned 409.
- **Solution.** Structural-key stitching only; on equal alias length the matcher returns None rather than the first alphabetical hit. Verdicts read from the body. The unobserved channel received its own verdict value instead of silent NO_PROOF. Infrastructure names quarantined from verdicts. Tests rebuilt to 83/83 with a kill-list drawn from live mail that produced 12/74 red before the fix. Outcome: NO_PROOF 33 to 19, proven 182/212 against a morning 177/217, rejections 9 against 7. A two-engine panel then found 2 further holes in the finished fix - an eternal body cache freezing empty bodies, and a cold pitch counted as an application on the same domain - both closed the same evening.
- **Pattern.** A test suite that has never been red against live data is a formatting check. The kill-list is the cheapest external verifier available.

### 5. One garbage vendor row absorbed 16 real employers' confirmations

- **Problem.** Within incident 4, a single company row named `ashby` was consuming confirmation mail belonging to other companies: 16 confirmations misattributed.
- **Cause.** `ashby` is an applicant-tracking vendor whose mail domain (its no-reply mail domain) serves hundreds of employers. The alias matched, and on ambiguity first-alphabetical won, so employer mail was credited to the infrastructure provider. 51 companies were additionally stuck on one verification code path from the same vendor.
- **Solution.** Infrastructure identities (`ashby`, `applied`) moved to quarantine and excluded from verdicts. Class family `stitching-by-text-not-by-key` registered in the class counter, which surfaced related cases dated 08-21 and 08-24.
- **Pattern.** An infrastructure identity that looks like an entity will absorb that entity's evidence. Identity keys must be structural, and ambiguity must resolve to "unknown".

### 6. The shadow run measured a rule similar to the shipped one

- **Problem.** The class "two parallel sessions receive the same order" had sat in the breakage journal since 08-13: 6 entries, 22 days with no mechanism. An archive scan found a storm of 41 sessions sharing one fingerprint inside a 46-minute window (13:58:04Z-14:44:26Z), against a journal estimate of 27-33.
- **Cause.** Sessions carried no fingerprint of their order and there was no registry of which order had already been claimed, so duplicates were caught only by eye, by the "later loses" convention, or by a harness write-guard. Confirming the gap: `clone_tourniquet.py`, registered in containment on 08-13, did not exist on disk. Three further causes were surfaced by an external panel of 3 engines before release: fingerprint truncation at 3000 characters merged different assignments sharing a preamble (174 phantoms of 688); the session marker was written even for one-word replies and then blindly blocked the real order; and a race between two simultaneous starts was unclosed. One engine additionally identified a measurement lie: our own shadow run, reporting zero false positives, was evaluating a rule similar to the shipped one rather than the shipped one, which fired 854 times on the same window.
- **Solution.** `clone_gate.py` built (hook/check/scan/bootstrap/selftest), wired as a UserPromptSubmit hook on the hub, advisory and fail-open. Rule: shout on a duplicate inside a 60-minute window, stay silent on legitimate periodics (>=5 runs older than the window, or a named exemption). Final measurement on the live registry: 11 firings over 7 days across 2085 orders, 1.5s per run. Tests 17/17 plus 6/6 mutants red. Bootstrap ingested 2084 historical orders in 4.4s. Canary registered on a second node, not yet applied.
- **Pattern.** A shadow that evaluates a reconstruction of the rule measures the reconstruction. The shadow must run the shipped artifact.

### 7. A watchdog ran green for a week while its executor existed in no scheduler

- **Problem.** The lead-conversation debt watchdog ran every 2 hours, reported green, and correctly identified debts: one peer waiting 31 hours, three more silent for weeks. No replies were sent.
- **Cause.** The executor routine's SKILL.md had been ready since 08-24, but the task itself was never registered in any scheduler. Underneath, 11 files of `scripts/_shared` vanished from disk at 13:54 the same day, breaking every routine in the family with ModuleNotFoundError - and the failure was masked because the wrappers compared the exit code against 7 instead of 1 and continued. Over one week the routine started 275 times, did real work once, and returned exit code 0 all 275 times. The class counter reported the 7th case in the family, oldest 08-11. A third constraint: `Register-ScheduledTask` with an S4U principal was refused with "Access is denied", so the routine only runs under an interactive principal.
- **Solution.** 11 files restored (3 tests green), `lead_thread_reply` registered twice a day, threads recorded in the lead-thread registry. The cause of the file disappearance is not proven and is recorded as an open question to the fleet rather than as a fix.
- **Pattern.** Watchdog liveness is not work liveness. A detector with no registered executor is an open relay, and an exit code says nothing about produced result.

### 8. The ACL emitter was found only after three blind manual repairs

- **Problem.** On the anchor node, the ACL mask on the shared imports directory kept losing effective rwx for the working user. Repaired by hand on 08-27, 08-29 and 08-30; it returned each time.
- **Cause.** POSIX mechanics. An hourly root cron (`7 * * * *`) runs `rsync -a` from a mirror into the live directory. `-a` implies `-p`; the source directories are mode 0755 with no ACLs; rsync issues `chmod 0755` on destination directories; `chmod` on a directory carrying an ACL recomputes `mask` from the group bits, giving `mask::r-x` and `user:anton:rwx #effective:r-x` while `default:mask::rwx` stays healthy - which is exactly the anomaly an earlier card could not explain. The flip occurs only on runs that actually transfer something. Evidence: on one file ctime 03:07:02 against mtime 03:05:57, the minute matching the cron minute.
- **Solution.** The rule of the third breakage triggered the emitter hunt instead of a fourth manual repair. The mask was deliberately left unrepaired in this pass so as not to mask the emitter again; a before/after observation on a transferring run was appended to the existing card as a named experiment. No duplicate cards created. Related: the `perm_canary` cron that should have caught this has no log at all and is dead; out of scope for the light profile and recorded as such.
- **Pattern.** One emitter can present as two unrelated classes - here both permission loss and code reversion came from the same hourly `rsync -a` without `-u`. And "rc=0, 0 files updated" is true and misleading at once: the mask is touched by the fact of a transfer, not by the file count.

### 9. A "seen" marker was used as a "done" marker in three independent pipes

- **Problem.** A nightly sweep of 10 batches of abandoned runs on the hub (670 sessions in queue, 138 strays) found one mechanism in three unrelated pipelines rather than ten separate defects.
- **Cause.** (1) `machine_bus.robot-done` is written at plan stage, before execution; an item without human confirmation the same day disappears from both plan and queue. (2) `_dr/queue/*/reminded.txt` is written after a single reminder and never re-checked: 26 research orders carry `reminded` and lack `delivered`, oldest 17 days. (3) The preference-sweep proposals file is overwritten rather than appended, so a day's proposals vanish silently. Overlap between the 09-02 plan (36 items) and the 09-03 plan (8 items): zero. Organisational root underneath: on 08-31, during the MSIX incident, one blanket order disabled 10 scheduler tasks including the entire repair rail (repair session, session unsticker, session-wait watchdog); last unsticker run 08-31 10:39. Since then `selfheal repairs` holds 28 system classes queued and all overdue, oldest 293 hours (~12 days); 27 of the 28 were already queued. In the in-app registry, 49 live tasks contain no task with "repair" in the name. Precedent for the inverse error: disabling the session reaper produced 138 zombie processes in three days.
- **Solution.** Family `seen-marker-used-as-done-marker` registered in the class counter (proved by a red/green run of `families_of()` before and after). The session deliberately did not re-enable the rails itself - storm risk while the incident is unresolved - and escalated the fork as a P0 card with the human as owner.
- **Pattern.** An acknowledgement is not a completion. A queue that drops unexecuted items rather than carrying them forward reports zero backlog by construction.

### 10. Identity was resolved inside the branch instead of above it

- **Problem.** The messaging ingest pipeline decided per chat and resolved the phone number inside each branch. A person with two accounts either failed the significance threshold, because 12 and 13 messages were counted separately rather than as 25, or received a second card. Separately, the coverage counter printed "395 notes from 1225 chats", which reads as coverage.
- **Cause.** Phone resolution sat below the branching. The name resolver knew only `contacts` keyed by chat jid, ignoring `jid_mapping` and frequent push-names. The counters measured containers (notes, chats) rather than content (messages).
- **Solution.** Phone lifted above the branching: 1202 private chats collapsed to 862 people, 136 of them assembled from more than one thread; nameless dialogues above 20 messages fell 24 to 19. Name resolution from 4 sources instead of 1. Three honest coverage lines replace one misleading line: real coverage 40.9% of all messages, 58.0% of textual; 63,816 messages cut by ceiling inside 37 notes; 1069 chats below threshold. Mirror-account chats with no unique content no longer spawn a second note (71 of 88 skipped by fact). Breaker suite 22/22 on kill-list, `+40` new checks. A two-engine panel surfaced the inverse risk of a strong key: 28 of 136 merges carry diverging signatures; review showed almost all are one person under different spellings, and the ambiguity is now printed as a line and written to card aliases rather than hidden.
- **Pattern.** An identity key applied after branching cannot merge across branches. A counter that measures containers overstates coverage by the container-to-content ratio.

### 11. A dedup judged an unfinished page load and nearly published nine duplicates

- **Problem.** The daily comment-reply routine had three defects; the third nearly produced up to 9 duplicate replies under one of Anton's own posts.
- **Cause.** (a) The platform pre-creates a "Reply to X" composer for every comment, so clicking Reply writes a mention into the existing node instead of creating one; the rule "exactly one new node must appear" could never fire. Live measurement: `prev=25, all=25, fresh=0`. (b) A comment can have multiple article copies in the DOM, and "not visible now" is not "absent" - the Reply button in the invisible copy is live. (c) The harvester's dedup passed verdict on a page whose expansion had not stabilised, and the platform returns names with a doubled space (a commenter name rendered with two spaces instead of one), so without normalisation the dedup went blind; replies-to-replies were also not counted. Same post across two days: 09-03 showed 14 comments and 0 answered, 09-04 showed 22 and 14. Two instrument lies were caught in the same pass: `pgrep` was absent from that shell so the background-run observer exited instantly, and `echo "EXIT=$?"` after a pipe measured `tail` rather than the script.
- **Solution.** Composer selection moved from browser JS into Python (`pick_composer()` + candidate/take helpers), so the rule is testable without a browser; targeting collects all article copies and scrolls the target into view; the harvester gained `expand_until_stable()` and `norm_name()`. Composer test 0/2 on 09-03 became 2/2 with full proof on 09-04; routine completion 6/20 (30%) became 2/2 (100%). Recorded as a class: a breakage that masks a more dangerous breakage looks like control. Known unresolved tension: the coverage gate demands 100% while the daily guard caps at 40 actions against 82 outstanding comments, so PASS is unreachable - recorded as a class rather than resolved by lowering the requirement.
- **Pattern.** A verdict taken before the observed surface has stabilised is a verdict about a different surface. When you fix a visible failure, check whether it was the only thing holding the line against an invisible one.

### 12. A login reported green and the rail was refused 14 seconds later

- **Problem.** The nightly sync for one account failed: login reported success at 02:57, the rail received 403 fourteen seconds later.
- **Cause.** The session existed only in the browser process's memory; cookies were never written to disk. `railc_login_a2.py` declared `outcome=ok` and exited 0 without ever checking the on-disk fact. Two inherited hypotheses were refuted in sequence by live evidence: "Crashed exit type prevents cookie flush" was refuted by a journal entry showing cookies on disk during a Crashed run on 09-03, and "the sync engine strips the profile via ignores" was refuted through the live ignores API plus a neighbouring profile in the same folder writing cookies normally. The corruption mechanism itself remains a hypothesis, explicitly not claimed as a proven cause.
- **Solution.** Old profile parked (not deleted), fresh profile created, session re-established via magic link; 16 cookies on disk after the fix. A single `verdict_persisted()` verdict wired into two doors with different contracts: at the session door a false-ok is exit 5; at the data door it is a warning without changing the exit code, so already-pulled data is not discarded. Tests 4/4 at each door, both showing red on the pre-fix copies. A sibling login path with a different cookie model was deliberately excluded from the class and given its own card. Class counter: 2nd dated case of `state-lives-only-in-process-memory`, mechanism threshold (3rd) not reached.
- **Pattern.** A green exit code with no on-disk check is a false-ok by construction. The routine gets green at the step that lies and fails at the next step, where the cause is no longer visible.

### 13. A provenance detector watched one third of the pipeline and went quiet at the damage

- **Problem.** The content factory published through a provenance gate whose bypass counter stayed calm during actual bypasses. Separately, the English lane was pointed at a platform where no account exists, and the publication ledger held no channel posts at all.
- **Cause.** (1) The dev.to rail was unviable - no account, and creating new accounts is forbidden by rule. (2) `pub_ledger` had never seen a channel post, and the 90-minute anti-flood gap is computed from that ledger, so the gap enforced nothing - 3rd dated case. (3) `approved_provenance.py` inspected only `approved/`, but a file published has already moved to `posted/`, so the detector was blind exactly where the damage lands. Measured visibility before the fix: 43%. Real bypasses 137 of 459 (approved 59, posted 75, expired 3), of which 75 were already published.
- **Solution.** Lane retargeted to the live EN channel (~169k subscribers), 58 files migrated, 11 posts overnight of which 8 went to EN; `posted/` grew 141 to 152. A PostToolUse hook (`tg_post_ledger.py`) on 4 telegram tools writes the publication fact through one door and only on result (a message id exists); an adversarial reviewer's proposal to write it in a PRE hook was rejected out loud, since a PRE hook cannot know the message id or the outcome. `approved_provenance.py` widened to the whole pipeline (approved/posted/expired). Teaser staleness policy verified live on both branches: 73 files swept to `expired/`. Test grid 45 tests, 43 green, 2 pre-existing red. Class counted as the 5th in the family; the 4th was found independently by a neighbouring node the same morning, learned through the shared class counter rather than through direct contact. The fail-closed decision on the 75 already-published bypasses was left to the human.
- **Pattern.** A detector must watch the damage set, not the staging set. A fact written from the attempt is not a fact.

### 14. A rollout gate judged a Python string as a file path; the shield had to be verify-only

- **Problem.** A login-persistence shield was built for the second browser on direct order. Two obstacles: the browser's Preferences file is MAC-signed, so direct edits trigger a "settings were reset" rollback; and the rollout registrar condemned the package because it could not find a file.
- **Cause.** The disconnections were server-side, not local - no clear-on-exit, no data-lifetime policy, no session-only cookie default - the same diagnosis as the previous day's Firefox case. The registrar's INVOKED-FILE gate extracted a path token from inside a Python string in the verification code (`pathlib.Path.home()/'.claude/...'`) and judged that substring as a missing file. Class counter: 10th case of `detector-judges-a-substring-not-an-action`; a P0 repair and a lint door already exist, so this is a new surface rather than a new mechanism.
- **Solution.** `chrome_login_shield.py` built as verify + backup (SQLite snapshot with `immutable=1`) + restore, never writing to the signed config; cross-OS schedule at 06:45 daily. Tests 12/12; live run on the laptop showed integrity ok, 2826 cookies in the backup, restore round-trip confirmed. Canary node applied, 5 nodes pending in inbox. ### 15. A false alarm about lost originals ran for the fourth time on the same broken method

- **Problem.** A standing alarm claimed research originals were being lost; a memory record dated 08-29 asserted 521 files lost.
- **Cause.** The check "no twin of this file in the vault, therefore lost" ignores that the canonical home of originals is not the vault but a byte-for-byte hub directory, with reconciliation running on A/B receipts. Reconciling 724 "deleted" files from the versions store against the canonical 1074-file directory returned NOT_IN_CANON = 0. - **Solution.** Breakage-journal entry closed as refuted; the 08-29 memory record closed by fields (`status=superseded/refuted`, `superseded_by`) rather than rewritten, per the close-by-fields rule; the related task card revised; notification dual-sent to the coordination channel. - **Pattern.** A repeated false alarm indicates a broken check, not changing data. A refuted record must be closed by machine-readable fields in every copy, or the next recall re-reads it as live.

### 16. A drip routine published and distributed a case unattended on night one; response was zero

- **Problem.** Building the first engine of the second mission: a 0-LLM detector over our own breakage journal that selects cases fit for a public casebook, writes them, publishes them and measures response.
- **Cause.** Not a failure - an architecture pass. The relevant defect: the first red-verification of the detector returned green because a shell `||` swallowed the exit code, making the redness check itself falsely green.
- **Solution.** A three-lens panel found a major hole in the leak gate before launch; the gate was hardened with 7 private-category regexes plus fleet host names and a ban on candidates lacking a source line; red-first test taken to 7/7. Candidates after hardening: 633 to 206. The routine then ran unattended at 04:26 on its first night, authored a new case, committed, pushed two cases and executed full distribution. Response measurement returned 0 on every axis (stars, traffic, clones, reproductions), read per existing rule as a distribution hole rather than a production hole; harvest date set to 09-17. Tests: detector 7/7 green with red exit 1; pulse 5/5 green with red exit 1. Open question left to the human: the detector threshold (upstream-linked cases only) is now out of fuel.
- **Pattern.** The redness check is itself an instrument and can be falsely green. Zero response with a working conveyor is a claim about distribution, and it needs a dated harvest before it becomes a verdict.

## Counterweight

Four boundaries were recorded as real rather than routed around. The booking service interface is not entered by the agent; the redirect switch is a human action. The pipeline API key requires an interactive console login the agent cannot perform, with three alternative doors closed. GitHub exposes no API to migrate stars or pins between repositories, which is what settled the monorepo question against consolidation. `change_directory` is blocked by the harness inside an isolated worktree, which no amount of logic resolves.

Two conclusions were retracted rather than defended. The morning claim that one vendor was unreachable from a node was withdrawn as over-broad: it rested on 2 Firefox surfaces out of 9 that exist. And the standing claim that 521 research originals were lost was closed as refuted after reconciliation returned zero.

One decision was made against our own interest: the session shield was withheld from publication because a tool that keeps third-party sessions alive reads badly from outside, and the optics matter more than the stars.
