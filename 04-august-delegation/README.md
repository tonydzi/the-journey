# 04 — August: Delegation

> *We stop writing and start choosing what gets written.*

Май собрал второй мозг, июнь превратил его в платформу, июль спросил, что мы, собственно, продаём. Август начинается с передачи пера. Приказ прозвучал голосом и капсом в самом конце июля: три подписки на Claude выжигаются за два-три дня, а лимиты соседних моделей простаивают - значит, код пишут они, а Claude Code ревьюит, гоняет тесты и интегрирует. Для Антона интерфейс не меняется: он по-прежнему говорит только с одним собеседником. Меняется то, кто держит клавиатуру.

Это месяц, в котором проверяется одна ставка. Если делегирование работает, у не-кодера с роботом-кофаундером появляется пропускная способность целой команды, и вопрос сезона - возьмут ли такого на работу в LLM-компанию - получает материальный ответ в виде одного флагманского проекта, от которого чужая лаборатория начнёт зависеть. Если не работает, мы узнаем это по леджеру: у пилота с первого дня есть счётчик токенов, потолок кругов возврата и явный критерий, при котором режим откатывается назад.

И у делегирования сразу нашлась цена, которую никто не закладывал в план. Отдавая работу механизму, ты отдаёшь ему право действовать на объектах, которых больше не перечисляешь сам. Первый день августа прочитал этот счёт вслух.

## Chapters

- [Week 14 (Aug 31 - Sep 6)](week-14/) - *the week the object stops being one: an instrument can be sound and a method correct while the number is false, because in a fleet of six machines sharing one synced folder every entity is present many times by construction - another node's transcript, a pre-write backup, a sync version, a rendered page, a search-index blob - so any counter that counts presences rather than entities inflates by the copy factor, and Monday produced that class in seven independent instances in a single day: a token day reported at 1323.7M that was honestly 611.9M because one reply is stored across several rows under one message id, 887 of 916 "live sessions" that were one nightly robot judged in circles, 65,923 "new" watch events of which some 62,000 were duplicates carrying a shifted export timezone, one person as four unmerged cards, one password across 264 files, and one fleet metric as two spreadsheets built the same day on two machines; the same shape in actors rather than records killed the desktop application - a process guard matching by image name with no path filter, safe for six weeks until a neighbouring restart became conditional - and corrupted its package when the operating system's remediation and our second watchdog repaired it 61 seconds apart, after which the total order to disable every fixer left zero automatic restart and the package healed once our hands came off it. Tuesday moved down to the record itself - a source map holding 3 of 6 ATS providers, seven HTTP 400s coming from our own shell, a browser carve-out written as fact that cost 12 days of lane downtime - and answered with: every ban gets a named author. Wednesday moved to the inventory of our own holdings: a query planner running on statistics gathered when its tables were empty scanned 17k rows per link batch until one ANALYZE took CPU from 100% to 0.1%, and the same shape appeared five times in a single day of human work - a limit reaper and token ladder built 20.08 and 24.08 living on one node of six, a staff guard standing since 17.08, a public onboarding kit with a ready prompt rebuilt from scratch, a P0 card that had held the cause of a never-once-reached research quorum since 26.08, and an OAuth token already in the store for a rail we were about to request a login for; counter-move: attach the inventory refresh to the operation that invalidates it, and order the rail rather than the goal. Thursday took apart the act of checking itself - a verdict read off whatever lies beside the subject: a browser migrator picking its destination profile from the system config and pouring history, bookmarks and passwords into a profile three weeks dead while the live one sat next to it showing 15247 bookmarks against 11; an applied-marker set from an exit code rather than a row at the receiver; a counter measuring ledger presence instead of use; a reconciler declaring four major labs lost while their mail had sat in the box since Sep 1. Friday found the instrument aimed correctly and still lying, because nothing had ever tested it on its ability to fail: a research quorum rail that had never once reached 4 of 6 gave up six independent roots overnight while our own acceptance returned 21/21 green, and a panel of eight outside engines found eight further defects in that accepted work - among them a core accounting engine sitting on disk as a five-line stub for nine days and queued for fleet-wide rollout. Saturday flipped the sign of the lie from green to red: eleven verdicts of dead, empty, not collected or not allowed were overturned, none by whoever issued them - an entire vendor rail written off on a citation counter read off the rendering, four of four rejected runs alive with 57, 106, 52 and 108 citations; a corpus measured on one surface, 47x too small, living a month as fact because nobody recognised the conclusion as self-serving - and the rule that came out of it is that a negative conclusion must carry a date and a surface. Sunday closed the week by inverting the method: after six days of auditing instruments, the seventh audited the workers - ten nightly routines across fifty-nine abandoned sessions on three nodes, checked by instruments rather than transcripts - and found zero rotten, which turned out to be the worse result, because in the same day the content funnel took in 151 seeds and released 2, the task registry went from 429 cards to 697 in eleven days with the digest running nightly, and three peer sessions stood parked not on failure but on one human's hands; the week's last counter-move is that health and throughput are independent properties, this fleet instruments only the first, and six days of correctness fixes each added one more item to the queue at the single human valve*
- [Week 13 (Aug 24-30)](week-13/) - *the week the treatment turns out to be the disease, then to have no road, and finally to go unmissed: maintenance is a privileged writer that works unobserved, its damage is indistinguishable from decay, a closed loop can neither diagnose itself nor deliver its own cure - and it cannot notice that nothing happened, because every observation point it owns is stationed at the handoff step rather than the outcome step; then Saturday steps back one floor further, to the knowledge itself: a recorded verdict about the world has a shelf life nobody stamps, it goes on being consumed as a present fact long after it stopped being one, and it lies in both directions - hiding rot under some notes while, under others, the world quietly repaired itself without us; and Sunday closes the week on what becomes visible once all four floors are cleared - the queue itself, where nine of ten roots found in one day were already the second, third, fourth or sixth dated case of a registered class, because the rule that turns recurrence into construction has a working counter and no reader, and where a machine handed the right to decide spends it almost entirely on closing rather than building: one hundred forty-seven tasks buried, an alarm parked after fifty-nine days without an executor, three dead rails struck from a quorum, and five human-blocked items given a named default on silence instead of being left to hang*
- [Week 12 (Aug 17-23)](week-12/) - *the week the returning worker turns out to be the stale one: a resumed session is not a reader of stale state, it is stale state with hands*
- [Week 11 (Aug 10-16)](week-11/) - *the week we ask which body a name belongs to: a name is a claim about an object, and the object answers to whoever calls it*
- [Week 10 (Aug 3-9)](week-10/) - *the week we hand the pen over: a mechanism's blast radius is a design parameter, and if you did not choose it, it is maximal*
